Dietary Supplement GMP

Dietary Supplement Labeling, Claims, and Adverse Event Reporting

Label elements, structure/function claims, and serious adverse event reporting.

What this course covers

A dietary supplement label does more than describe a product. It is a regulated document, and what it says about the product, and about what happens when a customer reports a problem, carries real compliance weight. This course covers the labeling, claims, and adverse event requirements every supplement firm must handle correctly.

The course begins with the label itself. Learners review the key elements: the statement of identity and net quantity, the Supplement Facts panel, the ingredient list including major allergens, and the name and place of business of the manufacturer, packer, or distributor. That contact point matters later, because it is where adverse event reports reach the firm.

Next, the course explains structure/function claims. Supplements may describe how an ingredient supports the normal structure or function of the body, but they may not claim to diagnose, mitigate, treat, cure, or prevent a disease. Learners see how names, imagery, and testimonials can imply a disease claim even when the wording looks safe, and why claims must be truthful and supported.

The course then covers the obligations that come with a claim. A firm must notify FDA no later than 30 days after first marketing the supplement with the claim, and the label must carry the required disclaimer in boldface. FDA does not approve the claim.

Finally, learners cover serious adverse event reporting. The manufacturer, packer, or distributor named on the label is the responsible person, and a serious adverse event report must reach FDA within 15 business days of receipt. The course closes with recordkeeping: adverse event records are kept for six years, and reports are reviewed as complaints under Part 111.

Who this course is for

  • Regulatory affairs, marketing and label review teams at supplement firms
  • Quality and complaint handling staff who receive customer reports
  • The manufacturer, packer or distributor named on a supplement label

What you'll learn

  • The key label elements: statement of identity, net quantity, Supplement Facts panel, ingredient list with major allergens, and the responsible firm’s name and place of business
  • What structure/function claims may and may not say, and how names, imagery and testimonials can imply a disease claim
  • The 30-day FDA notification and the boldface disclaimer that come with a claim
  • Who the responsible person is and the 15 business day deadline for serious adverse event reports
  • Adverse event recordkeeping for six years, and how reports are reviewed as complaints under Part 111

Why it matters

A dietary supplement label is a regulated document. What it claims, and how a firm handles a customer report of a problem, carries real compliance weight.
