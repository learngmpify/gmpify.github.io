Biopharmaceutical Essentials

How biologics manufacturing differs from small-molecule manufacturing, from upstream processing through characterization complexity.

What this course covers

This course covers how biopharmaceutical manufacturing differs fundamentally from small-molecule drug manufacturing, upstream and downstream processing basics including cell culture, harvest and purification, why biologics characterization is more complex than small-molecule analytical testing, and cold chain and stability considerations specific to biologic products.

Who this course is for

  • Staff transitioning into or supporting biologics manufacturing for the first time
  • Quality and regulatory staff who need biologics literacy alongside small-molecule experience

What you'll learn

  • How biopharmaceutical manufacturing differs fundamentally from small-molecule drug manufacturing
  • Upstream and downstream processing basics: cell culture, harvest and purification
  • Why biologics characterization is more complex than small-molecule analytical testing
  • Cold chain and stability considerations specific to biologic products
  • Where biopharmaceutical essentials connects to GMPify's stability-focused course content

Why it matters

A biologic is defined in large part by the process used to make it, unlike a small molecule, which means a manufacturing change can alter the product itself in ways small-molecule intuition doesn't anticipate. That single difference shapes nearly everything else about how biologics are controlled.
