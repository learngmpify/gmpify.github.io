Animal Feed GMP

Medicated Feed Drug Control, Cleanout and Carryover

Real FDA warning letters, and the Part 225 and Part 507 controls that prevent the same failures.

What this course covers

Medicated feed can fail in ways an animal owner never sees: the wrong drug in the mixer, too much drug in a batch, or residue left behind that reaches the next species. This course uses recent FDA warning letters to show how those failures happen in feed mills and what controls prevent them under 21 CFR Part 225 and the Part 507 preventive controls rule.

Learners begin with three real cases: a licensed mill that used monensin in place of a VFD drug, a mill with a lot containing more than three times the approved monensin level, and a mill with no flushing or sequencing between feeds. The course then covers drug identification and control in the mixing area, recording actual drug use by lot, and the daily comparison of actual and theoretical use under 225.42, with affected feed held until a discrepancy is resolved.

Next, learners compare the cleanout options in 225.65 (cleaning, flushing, sequencing, or an equally effective method), apply them across mixers, conveyors, bins and delivery trucks, and see why procedures and records matter. The course also shows how drug carryover, incorrect ingredients and mycotoxins belong in a Part 507 hazard analysis and food safety plan, with a qualified individual and validated controls.

Finally, learners cover invoices and weigh tickets that serve as labeling, VFD terms that must be followed, and how to answer an FDA warning letter within 15 working days with supporting records.

Who this course is for

  • Feed mill managers, quality and production staff
  • Qualified individuals who write and maintain the Part 507 food safety plan
  • Regulatory professionals who respond to FDA inspections and warning letters

What you'll learn

  • How recent warning letters show drug mix-ups, overdoses and carryover in feed mills
  • Drug identification and control in the mixing area, and recording actual drug use by lot
  • The daily comparison of actual and theoretical drug use under 225.42, and holding affected feed until a discrepancy is resolved
  • Cleanout options under 225.65: cleaning, flushing, sequencing or an equally effective method
  • How drug carryover, incorrect ingredients and mycotoxins belong in a Part 507 hazard analysis
  • Invoices and weigh tickets as labeling, VFD terms, and answering a warning letter within 15 working days

Why it matters

Medicated feed can fail in ways an animal owner never sees: the wrong drug in the mixer, too much drug in a batch, or residue that reaches the next species.
