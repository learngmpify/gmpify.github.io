Dietary Supplement GMP

Part 111 Production Records and Product Complaints

The master and batch records that prove a batch was made correctly, and the complaint system that follows it.

What this course covers

In dietary supplement manufacturing, a batch that is not documented cannot be shown to have been made correctly. This course covers the records that prove it, and the complaint system that checks product quality after it ships.

The course begins with how the two core production records work together. The master manufacturing record is written and followed for each unique formulation and batch size, and the batch production record is prepared every time a batch is manufactured. The batch record must accurately follow the master record and hold complete information on the production and control of the batch.

Next, learners review what 21 CFR 111.260 requires in a batch production record. This includes batch, lot, or control numbers, equipment and processing lines used, the identity and weight or measure of each component, actual and theoretical yield, cleaning and maintenance dates, monitoring and test results, and packaging and label information.

The course then covers real-time documentation and quality control review. Steps and decisions are recorded at the time they are performed, with dates and initials for weighing, adding, and verifying components. Quality control personnel review the record and test results, and approve, release, or reject the batch, any reprocessing or repackaging, and the packaged and labeled product.

Finally, learners cover product complaints under Subpart O. A qualified person reviews each complaint for a possible failure to meet specifications or other requirements, and quality control approves decisions on whether to investigate. A written record of every GMP-related complaint is kept, along with the product, batch if known, nature of the complaint, any reply, and investigation findings. The course also covers returned supplements and retention, 1 year past the shelf life date or 2 years past the last distribution if undated.

Who this course is for

  • Production and packaging staff who complete batch records
  • Quality control personnel who review records and release batches
  • Staff who receive, review and investigate product complaints

What you'll learn

  • How the master manufacturing record and the batch production record work together
  • What 21 CFR 111.260 requires in a batch production record
  • Real-time documentation, dates and initials, and quality control review and release
  • How product complaints are reviewed and investigated under Subpart O
  • Returned supplements and retention periods

Why it matters

A batch that is not documented cannot be shown to have been made correctly. The records, and the complaint system behind them, are what an inspector reads.
