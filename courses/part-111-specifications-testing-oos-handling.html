Dietary Supplement GMP

Part 111 Specifications, Testing, and Out-of-Specification Handling

How to set specifications, test against them, and document every failed result.

What this course covers

Under 21 CFR Part 111, every test result is measured against a written specification, and every failed result needs a documented decision. This course explains how supplement manufacturers set specifications, test against them, and respond when a result does not meet the standard.

The course begins with the specifications themselves. Components need an identity specification plus specifications for purity, strength, composition, and limits on contaminants. In-process steps, finished batches, packaging, labels, and products received for packaging and labeling each have their own. In-process specifications and their rationale are documented and approved by quality control.

Next comes testing. At least one appropriate test must verify the identity of each dietary ingredient before use, and a supplier certificate of analysis cannot replace it. Other components can be verified by testing or by relying on a qualified supplier’s COA. Finished batches are verified using a sound statistical sampling plan or by testing every batch, with scientifically valid methods and a documented, QC-approved basis for the tests chosen.

The course then covers what happens when a specification is not met. The default is rejection, unless quality control approves a permitted treatment, in-process adjustment, or reprocessing that protects quality and matches the master manufacturing record. A component that fails its identity specification must not be used in manufacturing.

Finally, learners see how material review and disposition works. QC conducts a documented review when a specification is not met, a batch deviates from the master manufacturing record, or an unanticipated occurrence happens. The decision is recorded at the time it is made, and repeat failures are investigated so the source of the problem is fixed.

Who this course is for

  • Quality control and laboratory personnel at dietary supplement firms
  • Quality assurance and regulatory professionals
  • Supplier qualification and purchasing teams

What you'll learn

  • The specifications Part 111 requires for components, in-process steps, finished batches, packaging and labels
  • Why each dietary ingredient needs an identity test, and when a qualified supplier COA can be relied on for other components
  • How finished batches are verified, using a statistical sampling plan or testing every batch
  • What happens when a specification is not met, and which treatments and reprocessing quality control can approve
  • How material review and disposition is documented, and how repeat failures are investigated

Why it matters

Every test result is measured against a written specification, and every failed result needs a documented decision.
