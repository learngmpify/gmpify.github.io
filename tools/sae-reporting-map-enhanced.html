SAE Reporting - Protecting Subjects Through Timely Safety Escalation

A serious adverse event report is the mechanism by which a single subject's experience becomes protective knowledge for every other subject in a trial, and every future patient who takes the product. ICH E2A defines the seriousness criteria, causality framework, and expedited reporting principles that apply across regions, while 21 CFR 312.32 and EU CTR 536/2014 set the specific regulatory timelines sponsors must meet. This map details the pathway from event identification through sponsor determination, expedited regulatory submission, and case follow-up to closure.

Fatal / Life-Threatening SUSAR

Unexpected, serious, related event that is fatal or life-threatening. Shortest reporting clock of any category.

Initial report: 7 calendar days
Follow-up: +8 calendar days

Other SUSAR

Unexpected, serious, related event that is not fatal or life-threatening. Standard expedited timeline.

Initial report: 15 calendar days
Follow-up: As available

Expected Serious AE

Serious and related, but consistent with the reference safety information. Not expedited.

Reporting route: Periodic (DSUR)

Non-Serious AE

Does not meet seriousness criteria. Captured in the safety database only.

Reporting route: Routine periodic only

SAE Reporting Timeline Requirements

Fatal or Life-Threatening SUSAR

Initial report to regulatory authorities within 7 calendar days of sponsor awareness, followed by a complete follow-up report within 8 additional calendar days.

21 CFR 312.32(c)(1)(i)

Other SUSAR

Initial report submitted within 15 calendar days of sponsor awareness, with follow-up reports as new information becomes available.

21 CFR 312.32(c)(1)(ii)

Expected Serious Adverse Events

Not subject to expedited timelines. Captured in the safety database and summarized in periodic aggregate reports.

ICH E2A Section III.A

Causality Assessment Categories (Overview)

Unrelated Event clearly explained by another cause, such as the underlying disease or a concomitant medication, with no plausible link to the investigational product.
Unlikely Temporal relationship exists but another explanation is more probable, and a causal link to the investigational product is not well supported.
Possible Reasonable temporal relationship exists and a causal link cannot be ruled out, though other explanations are also plausible.
Probable / Definite Strong temporal relationship, no more likely alternative explanation, and in definite cases a positive dechallenge or rechallenge supports the investigational product as the cause.

Regulatory Notification Recipients (Overview)

National regulatory authorities FDA IND safety report or the applicable national competent authority, per the jurisdiction in which the trial is conducted.
EU safety database SUSARs occurring in EU trials reported to the applicable EU/EEA pharmacovigilance database per EU CTR 536/2014.
IRB / Ethics Committee Notified of SUSARs and other safety information relevant to the ongoing evaluation of subject risk at the site.
Participating investigators All investigators across a multi-site trial informed of SUSARs that may affect their site's risk assessment or informed consent process.

SAE Reporting Quality Elements

Initial 24-Hour Site Notification Site reports the SAE to the sponsor within 24 hours of first becoming aware, regardless of completeness of information at that point.
Investigator Causality Assessment Documented, investigator-level judgment of the relationship between the event and the investigational product, recorded independent of the sponsor's later review.
Sponsor Medical Review Independent medical evaluation of seriousness, causality, and expectedness performed by qualified sponsor safety personnel.
SUSAR Determination Formal classification combining seriousness, causality, and expectedness to determine whether expedited reporting is triggered.
Expedited Regulatory Timelines 7-day and 15-day clocks met consistently, measured from the date the sponsor first becomes aware of a reportable event.
IRB / EC Notification Safety information relevant to subject risk communicated to the overseeing ethics body per protocol and applicable regulation.
Follow-Up Reporting New clinically significant information captured and submitted as supplemental reports until the case is resolved.
Aggregate Safety Reporting Individual case data rolled into periodic Development Safety Update Reports, providing cumulative trend context beyond single events.

Regulatory Text - Source Provisions

21 CFR 312.32(c)(1)(i)

The sponsor must notify FDA of any unexpected fatal or life-threatening suspected adverse reaction as soon as possible but in no case later than 7 calendar days after the sponsor's initial receipt of the information.

21 CFR 312.32(c)(1)(ii)

The sponsor must notify FDA of any suspected adverse reaction that is both serious and unexpected as soon as possible but in no case later than 15 calendar days after the sponsor determines that the information qualifies for reporting.

ICH E2A, Section II.C (paraphrased - refer to the official guideline for exact wording)

Expectedness is determined by whether the event is listed in the applicable reference safety information, such as the investigator's brochure, rather than by the event's clinical seriousness alone.

Common Inspection Findings - SAE Reporting

Illustrative of recurring themes seen across published FDA 483s and warning letters. Not tied to a single specific citation.
Expedited reporting clock started late Timeline measured from a document date rather than from the date the sponsor first received sufficient information to trigger the reporting obligation.
Causality assessment not independently performed Sponsor safety review adopting the site's causality assessment without a documented independent medical evaluation.
Not all participating sites notified Safety information distributed to some but not all investigators across a multi-site trial following a SUSAR determination.
Follow-up information not pursued Case left open with no evidence of active follow-up to obtain missing information needed to complete the safety assessment.

Best Practice Guidance

Define awareness date in the safety management plan Document precisely what constitutes sponsor awareness for clock-start purposes, so the same standard applies consistently across all sites and reviewers.
Separate site and sponsor causality assessments Keep the investigator's causality judgment and the sponsor's independent medical review as distinct, separately documented steps rather than one carrying forward the other.
Maintain a live distribution list for safety notifications Keep an up-to-date list of all investigators and IRBs across a multi-site trial so SUSAR notifications reach every site without manual reconstruction each time.
Track open cases against a follow-up cadence Review open SAE cases on a fixed cycle to confirm active follow-up is occurring, rather than waiting for the next scheduled report to notice a stalled case.

SAE vs AE

An adverse event is any untoward medical occurrence in a subject. A serious adverse event meets one or more specific seriousness criteria, such as death, hospitalization, or life-threatening risk. Seriousness, not severity, determines the regulatory reporting pathway.

Expedited vs periodic reporting

Expedited reporting applies to SUSARs and follows fixed regulatory clocks measured in days. Periodic reporting, such as the DSUR, aggregates all safety data including expected events on a scheduled annual or protocol-defined cycle.
