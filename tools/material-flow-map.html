Material Flow - Designing Movement to Prevent Mix-Ups by Default

Material flow describes the controlled, unidirectional path materials take through a facility, from receipt through quarantine, testing, release, storage, dispensing, and into production, with rejected and returned material flowing separately. EU GMP Chapters 3 and 5 and 21 CFR 211.80 through 211.87 collectively expect this flow to be designed so that the physical layout and status labeling system make an accidental mix-up structurally difficult, not merely something personnel are trained to avoid through vigilance alone.

ICH Q7

Quarantine / Under Test

Material received but not yet tested and released, physically segregated and clearly labeled to prevent inadvertent use before disposition.

Access: Restricted, no production use

Approved / Released

Material that has passed all required testing and quality unit review, formally released and available for use in production.

Access: Available for production use

Rejected

Material that failed testing or quality review, segregated from approved material and pending disposition such as return or destruction.

Access: Segregated, no production use

Returned / Recalled

Material returned from a customer or recalled from the field, held separately pending investigation and disposition decision.

Access: Segregated, investigation pending

Material Status Labeling Requirements

Unambiguous Visual Status

Status distinguishable at a glance, commonly through color-coded labels or tags, so personnel can identify material status without reading detailed documentation.

EU GMP Chapter 5

System and Physical Status Match

Inventory system status and physical label status kept synchronized at all times, with any discrepancy investigated immediately upon discovery.

21 CFR 211.82

Full Traceability to Source

Every material lot traceable back to its original receipt, supplier, and all testing and status changes throughout its life in the facility.

21 CFR 211.84

Unidentified or Mislabeled Material Response Steps

Step 1 - Immediately quarantine the material Physically segregate and label the material as quarantined the moment an identification or labeling discrepancy is discovered.
Step 2 - Halt any related production activity Stop any batch operation using material from the same receipt or lot until the discrepancy is resolved.
Step 3 - Investigate the point of failure Trace back through the material's documented history to determine where the identification or labeling error occurred.
Step 4 - Assess batch impact Determine whether any batch already used material from the affected lot, and evaluate the impact on those batches.
Step 5 - Correct, re-verify, and document Correct the identification or labeling error, re-verify material identity through testing if warranted, and fully document the event.

Storage Condition Requirements

Temperature and humidity monitoring Storage areas continuously monitored against the specific condition requirements of the materials held within them.
Segregation by status and material type Physical segregation maintained not only by approval status but also between materials that could be confused due to similar appearance or packaging.
Pest and environmental control Warehouse areas maintained under an active pest control programme and protected from environmental conditions that could compromise material quality.
Restricted access to quarantine and rejected areas Physical or procedural access controls limiting who can move material into or out of quarantine and rejected storage areas.

Unidirectional Flow Design Elements

Segregated Receiving and Shipping Physically separate receiving and shipping areas preventing incoming and outgoing material streams from crossing paths.
Dedicated Quarantine Area Distinct, clearly demarcated quarantine zone separate from released material storage, minimizing the chance of accidental use.
Controlled Dispensing Booth or Area Dedicated dispensing area, often with local exhaust or laminar airflow, where materials are weighed and verified before entering production.
Personnel and Material Flow Separation Personnel movement paths designed separately from material movement paths where practical, reducing cross-contamination and mix-up opportunities.
Color-Coded Status Labeling System Consistent color scheme applied facility-wide so status is immediately recognizable regardless of which area or shift is involved.
FIFO/FEFO Rotation Enforcement Physical layout and system controls supporting consistent first-in-first-out or first-expired-first-out material rotation.
Cleaning Between Material Types Defined cleaning procedures for shared handling equipment, such as pallet jacks or scales, between different material types or lots.
Separate Reject and Return Flow Path Dedicated physical route and documentation trail for rejected and returned material, kept distinct from the approved material pathway at every point.

Never do this

Store quarantined and released material in the same physical area, relying on labels alone to prevent mix-up. Allow inventory system status to lag behind physical status changes. Dispense materials without second-person verification of identity and quantity. Route rejected material through the same physical path as approved material.

Material flow vs personnel flow

Material flow and personnel flow are related but distinct design considerations. Materials move according to their quality status and processing stage; personnel move according to gowning level and area classification. A well-designed facility addresses both without assuming that controlling one automatically controls the other.

Quarantine vs rejected

Quarantine status means disposition has not yet been determined; the material could still be released pending successful testing. Rejected status means a disposition decision has already been made against the material's use. Confusing the two statuses risks either premature use or unnecessary disposal.
