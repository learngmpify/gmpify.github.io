Batch Record Review - The Complete, Contemporaneous Story of a Batch

The batch production record is the primary documentary evidence that a specific batch was manufactured according to its approved procedure, and its review is the final gate confirming that evidence is complete and coherent before the batch can be released. 21 CFR 211.188 requires batch production and control records for each batch, 211.192 requires investigation of any unexplained discrepancy, and 211.194 sets the standard for laboratory records supporting the batch. Together they expect a record built contemporaneously, step by step, rather than reconstructed from memory once production has already finished.

First-Person Review (Production)

Initial review performed by production personnel or supervisor confirming the record is complete and internally consistent immediately after execution.

Timing: Immediately post-execution

Second-Person Verification

Independent verification of critical steps performed contemporaneously by a second qualified individual during execution, not only at final review.

Timing: Contemporaneous with execution

Exception / Deviation Review

Focused review of any deviation, blank field, or discrepancy identified during the record, requiring documented investigation before proceeding.

Trigger: Any identified discrepancy

Final Quality Unit Review

Comprehensive final review by the quality unit, independent of production, forming the basis for the batch disposition decision.

Timing: Before batch release

Missing or Incomplete Documentation Response Steps

Step 1 - Identify the specific gap Determine precisely which field, step, or verification is missing or incomplete in the record.
Step 2 - Determine whether contemporaneous completion is still possible Assess whether the missing information can still be legitimately obtained, or whether the window for contemporaneous documentation has passed.
Step 3 - Never backfill from memory as if contemporaneous Where the record cannot be completed contemporaneously, address the gap through a documented deviation rather than an undated retrospective entry.
Step 4 - Assess batch quality impact Evaluate whether the missing documentation raises a genuine question about the batch's quality or whether it is a documentation-only gap.
Step 5 - Document root cause and corrective action Investigate why the gap occurred and implement corrective action addressing the underlying documentation practice.

Data Integrity Requirements (ALCOA+)

Attributable and legible Every entry attributable to a specific, identifiable individual and legible enough to be read without ambiguity.
Contemporaneous and original Entries made at the time the activity occurred, preserved in their original form rather than transcribed or recreated later.
Accurate and complete Recorded values accurate to what actually occurred, with no field left blank without a documented explanation.
Consistent, enduring, and available Data consistent across related records, retained durably for the required period, and available for review or inspection when needed.

Batch Record Content Elements

Master vs Executed Record Distinction Clear distinction maintained between the approved master batch record template and the specific executed record for an individual batch.
Critical Step Sign-Off Explicit sign-off required at each critical step, distinct from routine step completion, reflecting the elevated verification that step demands.
Yield and Component Reconciliation Complete reconciliation of components used and product yield obtained, documented and compared against theoretical expectations.
Deviation Cross-Reference Any deviation occurring during the batch cross-referenced by number within the batch record, linking the two records together.
Equipment and Material Traceability Specific equipment identifiers and material lot numbers used documented, supporting full traceability for the batch.
Environmental Data Attachment Relevant environmental monitoring data for the batch's manufacturing period attached or referenced within the record where applicable.
Electronic vs Paper Record Controls Electronic batch records subject to the same data integrity expectations as paper, plus audit trail and electronic signature requirements.
Review Timeliness Standard Defined internal timeline for completing batch record review, preventing indefinite delay between batch completion and release decision.

Regulatory Text - Source Provisions

21 CFR 211.188

Batch production and control records shall be prepared for each batch of drug product produced and shall include complete information relating to the production and control of each batch, documented at the time of performance.

21 CFR 211.192

All drug product production and control records, including those for packaging and labeling, shall be reviewed and approved by the quality control unit to determine compliance with all established, approved written procedures before a batch is released or distributed.

Best Practice Guidance

Require a documented reason for every blank or N/A field Build a standing rule into batch record procedures that no field may remain blank without either a value or an explicit, signed N/A justification.
Use system timestamps to support contemporaneous documentation claims Where electronic batch records are in use, rely on system-generated timestamps rather than manually entered dates to substantiate contemporaneous entry.
Set an internal review timeliness target and track it Define a specific target number of days from batch completion to final QA disposition, and monitor actual performance against that target.
Train production supervisors to catch gaps before QA sees the record Strengthen the first-level supervisor review specifically to catch blank fields and inconsistencies, reducing the volume of issues surfacing only at final QA review.

Never do this

Complete batch record entries after the fact and represent them as contemporaneous. Allow the same person to both execute and independently verify a critical step. Release a batch with an unresolved discrepancy or unexplained blank field. Treat electronic batch records as exempt from the correction and traceability standards applied to paper.

Contemporaneous vs retrospective documentation

Contemporaneous documentation is recorded at the moment the activity happens, capturing what was actually observed. Retrospective documentation, reconstructed from memory after the fact, cannot provide the same evidentiary value and should never be presented as if it were contemporaneous.

First-person vs second-person review

First-person review is the individual confirming their own work is complete. Second-person verification is an independent check by someone else, providing a genuine control against individual error or oversight that self-review alone cannot provide.
