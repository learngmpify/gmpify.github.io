Monitoring Visits - The Sponsor's Window Into Site Conduct

Monitoring is the process by which a sponsor confirms that a trial is being conducted, recorded, and reported in accordance with the protocol, GCP, and applicable regulation, and that subject rights and safety are being protected. ICH E6(R2) requires a monitoring approach appropriate to the specific risks of the trial, ranging from on-site visits to centralized, risk-based methods. This map details the pathway from monitoring plan development through pre-study, initiation, routine, and closeout visits, and the follow-up cycle that closes each finding.

Monitoring Visit Types and Purpose

Pre-Study and Initiation Visits

Confirm site capability before selection and readiness before enrollment. Focused on infrastructure, staff training, and document completeness.

ICH E6(R2) Section 5.18

Routine Monitoring Visits

Ongoing verification of source data, protocol adherence, and product accountability, at a frequency set by the risk-based monitoring plan.

ICH E6(R2) Section 5.18.3

Closeout Visits

Final confirmation that all outstanding items are resolved and the site's records are complete before its participation formally ends.

Source Data Verification Approach (Overview)

100% SDV Every data point verified against source documents. Resource-intensive, typically reserved for high-risk trials or critical endpoints.
Targeted / risk-based SDV Verification focused on critical data points, such as eligibility, primary endpoints, and safety data, with reduced verification elsewhere.
Centralized and remote monitoring Statistical review of aggregated data across sites to identify outliers and trends, supplementing or partially replacing on-site visits.
Sampling strategy documentation The chosen approach and its rationale documented in the monitoring plan, so the level of verification applied is traceable and justified.

Common Monitoring Finding Categories (Overview)

Source documentation gaps Case report form entries without a supporting source document, or source records that are incomplete or not contemporaneous.
Protocol deviations Departures from the approved protocol identified during source review, ranging from visit window issues to eligibility questions.
Product accountability discrepancies Dispensing or return records that do not reconcile with the investigational product accountability log.
Regulatory document currency Expired approvals, outdated delegation logs, or missing training records identified in the site's regulatory binder.

Monitoring Visit Quality Elements

Risk-Based Monitoring Plan Documented plan defining monitoring approach, visit frequency, and SDV sampling strategy scaled to trial and site risk.
Visit Frequency Justification Rationale for the chosen visit cadence, tied to enrollment rate, protocol complexity, and observed site performance.
Source Data Verification Case report form data checked against source documents per the defined sampling strategy at every routine visit.
Regulatory Binder Review Site regulatory documents, including approvals, delegation logs, and training records, confirmed current at each visit.
Investigational Product Accountability Dispensing, storage, and return records reconciled against the accountability log at every applicable visit.
Informed Consent Verification Consent documentation confirmed complete and properly dated relative to study procedures for every enrolled subject.
Corrective Action Tracking Findings tracked from identification through documented, verified resolution rather than closed on the site's word alone.
Visit Report Documentation Written report and site confirmation letter completed for every visit, forming part of the trial master file record.

Regulatory Text - Source Provisions

ICH E6(R2), Section 5.18.3

The sponsor should develop a systematic, prioritized, risk-based approach to monitoring clinical trials, with the flexibility to use a combination of on-site and centralized monitoring, as appropriate.

ICH E6(R2), Section 5.18.4

The purpose of monitoring is to verify that the rights and well-being of human subjects are protected, the reported trial data are accurate, complete, and verifiable from source documents, and the conduct of the trial complies with the approved protocol, GCP, and applicable regulatory requirements.

21 CFR 312.50 (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

Sponsors are responsible for selecting qualified investigators, providing them with necessary information, ensuring proper monitoring of the investigation, and ensuring that the investigation is conducted in accordance with the general investigational plan and protocols in the IND.

Common Inspection Findings - Monitoring Visits

Illustrative of recurring themes seen across published FDA 483s and warning letters. Not tied to a single specific citation.
Visit reports not completed or completed late Monitoring visits conducted with no corresponding written report on file, or reports finalized months after the visit occurred.
Findings repeated across consecutive visits The same finding noted at multiple visits with no evidence that a corrective action was implemented or verified in between.
SDV sampling rationale undocumented A reduced or targeted verification approach applied at the site with no documented justification in the monitoring plan.
Escalation pathway not followed Persistent site performance concerns identified in visit reports with no evidence of escalation per the sponsor's defined process.

Best Practice Guidance

Finalize visit reports within a fixed window Set an internal target, such as within a set number of business days, for completing the monitoring visit report so findings are documented while still fresh.
Verify closure, do not just record it Confirm a corrective action independently at the following visit rather than accepting the site's statement that an issue has been resolved.
Revisit the monitoring plan as risk changes Adjust visit frequency and SDV sampling when enrollment accelerates, a safety signal emerges, or a site's performance pattern changes.
Keep the escalation pathway concrete and time-bound Define specific triggers and timeframes for escalating a site concern, rather than leaving escalation to individual monitor judgment alone.

On-site vs centralized monitoring

On-site monitoring involves a monitor physically reviewing records at the site. Centralized monitoring uses statistical analysis of data across sites to identify risk signals remotely. Most modern monitoring plans blend both approaches based on trial risk.

Monitoring vs auditing

Monitoring is an ongoing sponsor function verifying day-to-day trial conduct at the site level. Auditing is an independent, periodic sponsor quality function assessing whether monitoring and site processes themselves are working as intended.
