Production Controls - Consistent Devices, Every Batch

Under the Quality Management System Regulation, the former production and process controls section, 21 CFR 820.70, is replaced by ISO 13485:2016 clause 7.5, which Part 820 incorporates by reference. Clause 7.5 requires production to be planned, carried out, monitored, and controlled, with validated processes, identification, traceability, and a record for each device or batch. 21 CFR 820.10(b) adds unique device identification and tracking requirements. This map follows the pathway from planning through execution, identification and traceability, and release.

Control of Production

Production and service provision are planned, carried out, monitored, and controlled so devices meet specification, under documented procedures and work instructions.

Basis: Clause 7.5.1

Process Validation

Processes whose output cannot be fully verified by later inspection or test are validated to show they achieve planned results consistently.

Basis: Clause 7.5.6

Identification and Traceability

Product is identified through production, and traceability procedures are kept. FDA adds unique device identification and tracking rules.

Basis: Clauses 7.5.8, 7.5.9; 820.10(b)

Production Record

A record for each device or batch provides traceability and identifies the quantity manufactured and the quantity approved for distribution.

Basis: Clause 7.5.1

Process Control Types (Overview)

Validated Processes

Special processes whose output cannot be fully verified, such as sterilization or sealing, validated before use and revalidated as required.

ISO 13485:2016 7.5.6

Verified Processes

Processes whose results are confirmed by inspection or test against acceptance criteria.

ISO 13485:2016 7.5.1, 8.2.6

Monitored Parameters

Process and environmental parameters monitored and recorded during production.

ISO 13485:2016 7.5.1, 6.4

Production Control Quality Elements

Documented Procedures and Instructions Work is defined, controlled, and available at the point of use.
Validated Special Processes Processes that cannot be fully verified are validated.
Qualified Equipment and Environment Infrastructure and environment support product quality.
Competent Personnel People are trained for the tasks they perform.
Process Monitoring Parameters and characteristics are monitored as planned.
Identification and Traceability Product and status are identified, and traceability is maintained.
Complete Production Records Each device or batch has a verified and approved record.
Controlled Release Release follows completed activities and documented acceptance.

Regulatory Text - Source Provisions

ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.5.1, as incorporated by 21 CFR 820.10(a) (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

Production and service provision are planned, carried out, monitored, and controlled to ensure product conforms to specification. Controls include documented procedures and methods, suitable infrastructure and work environment, monitoring and measurement of process parameters and product characteristics, and defined release, delivery, and post-delivery activities. For each device or batch, a record provides traceability and identifies the quantity manufactured and the quantity approved for distribution, and is verified and approved.

ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.5.6 (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

Processes for production and service provision are validated where the output cannot be, or is not, verified by later monitoring or measurement. Validation demonstrates the ability of these processes to consistently achieve planned results. Documented procedures cover validation, including the validation of software used in production, before initial use and after changes, proportionate to the risk.

21 CFR 820.10(b)(1) and (b)(2) (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

For Clause 7.5.8, Identification, the manufacturer must document a system to assign unique device identification in accordance with Part 830. For Clause 7.5.9.1, Traceability, the manufacturer must document procedures for traceability in accordance with Part 821, if applicable.

Common Inspection Findings - Production and Process Controls

Illustrative of recurring themes seen in FDA inspections of device production and process controls, including under the former Quality System Regulation, which are expected to continue under the QMSR. Not tied to a single specific citation.
Process changes made without assessment Parameters or equipment are changed with no evaluation of whether revalidation is needed.
Special processes not validated, or validation incomplete No documented validation, or validation that does not cover worst-case conditions or revalidation triggers.
Work instructions not followed or out of date Operators use uncontrolled copies, or practice differs from the written instruction.
Incomplete production records Records missing signatures, quantities, or traceability, yet product is released.

Best Practice Guidance

Link process changes to a change review Require a documented assessment of revalidation need for every change to a validated process.
Define worst-case conditions in validation Include challenge conditions and record the rationale for the chosen acceptance criteria.
Make work instructions usable at the point of work Keep one controlled version at the workstation, written with the operators.
Review records before release Use a checklist so quantities, signatures, and traceability are confirmed prior to release.

Never do this

Run a special process that has not been validated. Change a validated process without assessing the need for revalidation. Let nonconforming product move on without identification. Release product before the batch record is complete. Use production software without validating it.

Validate or verify

If the result of a process cannot be fully verified by later inspection or test, validate the process. If it can be verified, verify it against defined acceptance criteria. Record which route was chosen and why.

What changed under the QMSR

The former production and process controls section, 21 CFR 820.70, is replaced by ISO 13485 clause 7.5. 21 CFR 820.10(b) adds unique device identification under Part 830 and, where applicable, traceability under Part 821.

Key standards

ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.5.1 - control of production. Clause 7.5.6 - validation of processes. Clauses 7.5.8 and 7.5.9 - identification and traceability. 21 CFR 820.10(a) and (b) - QMS requirement and FDA-specific requirements.
