New Supplier Identified - Formal Qualification Required Before Use in GMP Manufacturing

21 CFR 211.84 permits reliance on a supplier's Certificate of Analysis only if the manufacturer periodically validates the supplier's testing. EU GMP Chapter 5.27 and 5.29 require documented qualification of all starting material suppliers and mandatory audits of active substance manufacturers. Using an unqualified supplier for GMP materials is a direct 21 CFR 211.84 violation regardless of the analytical quality of the material received. Supplier qualification is not a purchasing process - it is a patient safety process.

21 CFR 211.84 and EU GMP Chapter 5.27, 5.29 - regulatory basis

A manufacturer is expected to establish the reliability of a supplier's analyses through documented qualification before relying on that supplier's certificate of analysis for material release, with active substance manufacturers specifically subject to a mandatory audit requirement that a desk-based review alone does not satisfy.

⚠ Illustrative case pattern - missing quality agreement

A contract manufacturer supplying a critical intermediate has been qualified, audited and added to the Approved Supplier List. Materials have been received and used in GMP production for over a year. At inspection, the investigator requests the quality agreement governing change notification and deviation handling responsibilities between the two firms. None exists - only a commercial purchase agreement covering price, quantity and delivery terms. The site has been operating a functioning qualification programme without the one document EU GMP Chapter 7 and FDA's 2016 guidance both require. This is the single most frequently cited supplier qualification deficiency in FDA warning letters and EMA inspections in 2025 and 2026.

High Risk Suppliers

On-site audit required before approval. Annual re-audit cycle. Full quality agreement with all elements mandatory. Intensive incoming material testing programme. Any FDA warning letter, import alert or EU GMP non-compliance certificate in the last three years requires enhanced assessment before qualification.

API manufacturers  ·  Critical excipient suppliers  ·  Contract manufacturers  ·  Contract testing labs  ·  Primary packaging suppliers

Medium Risk Suppliers

Desk-based assessment with documented review of quality system documentation, regulatory status and COA history. On-site audit may be required based on risk assessment conclusion. Quality agreement required. Periodic re-qualification at defined intervals.

Secondary excipient suppliers  ·  Indirect contact packaging  ·  Cleaning agent suppliers  ·  Reference standard suppliers

Low Risk Suppliers

Qualification questionnaire and documentation review. No on-site audit required unless risk assessment indicates otherwise. Simplified quality agreement or technical agreement acceptable. Annual COA review. Re-qualification at extended intervals.

Non-contact consumable suppliers  ·  Laboratory consumables  ·  Non-critical utilities  ·  Office/admin supplies for GMP areas
Phase 1
Identification and Risk Assessment
1Identify supplier and material scope
Document the proposed supplier, materials or services, and intended use. Identify all products and batches that would rely on this supplier. Confirm current Manufacturing Authorisation, registration or licence. Check FDA warning letter database, import alert list and EU GMP non-compliance database before proceeding.
21 CFR 211.84(a) and EU GMP Chapter 5.27 - regulatory basis

Materials used in GMP manufacturing are expected to originate only from suppliers whose licensure, registration and regulatory standing have been confirmed and documented before the material enters the supply chain, not verified retrospectively after use has begun.

21 CFR 211.84(a)  ·  EU GMP Chapter 5.27  ·  FDA Warning Letter database
↓
2Risk assessment and tier assignment
Conduct formal risk assessment using ICH Q9(R1) 2023 methodology: material criticality, supplier GMP status and history, supply chain complexity, availability of alternatives and consequence of supply failure. Assign High, Medium or Low tier with documented rationale.
✓ Best practice - rationale that survives a tier challenge

Document why a supplier was assigned to its tier with the same rigor you would use to justify a worst-case product selection in cleaning validation. "Excipient supplier, low criticality" is not a rationale. A rationale references the specific criticality assessment, the supplier's regulatory history check, and the consequence-of-failure analysis that produced the tier decision.

ICH Q9(R1) 2023  ·  USP 1083  ·  ICH Q10 Section 3.2.3
↓

YES → Enhanced assessment required NO → Phase 2

⚠ Active regulatory action - do not approve without QA director sign-off
A supplier with a current FDA warning letter, active import alert or EU GMP non-compliance certificate presents elevated risk to your supply chain and regulatory standing. Enhanced assessment including senior QA and regulatory affairs review is required. Approval must include documented risk acceptance at QA director or higher level with a defined monitoring and exit strategy.
Phase 2
Qualification Assessment
3Send qualification questionnaire
Structured questionnaire covering quality system certification, GMP compliance history, manufacturing controls, specifications and testing capabilities, change control commitments, deviation and CAPA systems, complaint handling and recall capability. Responses must be assessed, not just collected.
✓ Best practice - read for evasion, not just completion

A fully completed questionnaire with vague, generic answers to specific questions is a disqualifying signal, not a passing result. Compare specific answers against what a genuinely mature quality system would say - a supplier that cannot describe its own deviation escalation thresholds in concrete terms likely does not have one.

21 CFR 211.84(d)  ·  EU GMP Chapter 5.29  ·  USP 1083
↓
4Review quality system documentation
Request and review GMP certificate or ISO 9001 certification, Site Master File, recent audit reports, regulatory inspection outcomes from the last three years, technical data sheets, COA examples and analytical method summaries.
EU GMP Chapter 5.29 - regulatory basis

Documented review of an active substance manufacturer's GMP compliance status is expected before that manufacturer is approved as a supplier, with the review current enough to reflect the manufacturer's most recent regulatory inspection outcomes.

EU GMP Chapter 5.29  ·  21 CFR 211.84  ·  ICH Q7
↓
Does risk tier require on-site audit?
YES - High risk → Step 5 NO - Med/Low → Step 6
↓
5Conduct on-site GMP audit
On-site audit covering facility condition, manufacturing controls, quality management system, laboratory controls including OOS and method validation, personnel training, deviation and CAPA effectiveness, change control and cleaning validation for shared equipment. Critical findings prevent approval until resolved.
⚠ Illustrative case pattern - desk review substituted for mandatory audit

A site qualifies an API manufacturer using a thorough desk-based assessment: certificates, questionnaires, and a strong regulatory history check, but no on-site audit component. EU GMP Chapter 5.27 requires active substance manufacturers to be audited, and a desk-based assessment alone does not satisfy this for API suppliers regardless of how comprehensive it is. This gap is identified as one of the most frequently exploited weaknesses in EU GMP supplier qualification programmes - not because the desk review was inadequate, but because it was the wrong tool for this supplier category entirely.

EU GMP Chapter 5.27  ·  21 CFR 211.84  ·  ICH Q10 Section 3.2.3
↓
6Incoming material qualification testing
Full identity, assay, purity and impurity testing against release specifications regardless of the supplier COA. Reduced testing against COA only permitted after supplier testing reliability has been validated through appropriate means at appropriate intervals.
21 CFR 211.84(d)(1) and (d)(2) - regulatory basis

Initial qualification requires full analytical testing of the material regardless of the supplier's certificate of analysis. Transition to reduced testing against the COA is conditioned on documented validation of the supplier's analytical reliability, not on the supplier's certification status alone.

21 CFR 211.84(d)(1) and (d)(2)  ·  EU GMP Chapter 5.31
Phase 3
Approval and Quality Agreement
7Prepare supplier qualification report
Document the complete outcome: risk assessment and tier with rationale, questionnaire assessment, documentation review findings, audit report summary and CAPA status, incoming testing results, and overall conclusion. QA review and approval required before addition to the ASL.
21 CFR 211.84 and ICH Q10 Section 3.2.3 - regulatory basis

The qualification report is expected to function as the complete evidence package supporting the approval decision - a decision cannot rest on individual review elements that were never consolidated into a single QA-reviewed conclusion.

21 CFR 211.84  ·  ICH Q10 Section 3.2.3  ·  EU GMP Chapter 5
↓
Does the qualification assessment support supplier approval?
YES → Step 8 NO → Do not approve
↓ YES
8Negotiate and execute quality agreement
Required for all high and medium risk suppliers. Must cover material specifications, change control and notification, deviation and CAPA responsibilities, complaint handling, audit rights, recall procedures and GMP compliance maintenance obligations.
✓ Best practice - a purchase agreement is not a quality agreement

Check explicitly whether the document governing this relationship addresses change notification timelines, audit rights and recall cooperation - not just price and delivery terms. A commercial purchase agreement, however detailed on commercial terms, does not satisfy EU GMP Chapter 7 or FDA's 2016 guidance if it is silent on these quality-specific obligations.

EU GMP Chapter 7  ·  FDA Contract Manufacturing Guidance 2016  ·  21 CFR 211.84
↓
9Add to Approved Supplier List
Add with supplier name and address, materials approved, risk tier, qualification date, next re-qualification date, quality agreement reference, approved contacts and any conditions of approval. ASL is a controlled document requiring QA approval for all changes.
21 CFR 211.84(a) and EU GMP Chapter 5.27 - regulatory basis

The Approved Supplier List is expected to function as the controlling record of which suppliers may provide GMP materials - materials from a supplier not reflected on the current, QA-approved ASL are not authorised for use regardless of the analytical quality of the material itself.

21 CFR 211.84(a)  ·  EU GMP Chapter 5.27  ·  ICH Q10
✓ APPROVED

Added to ASL. Quality agreement executed. Ongoing monitoring initiated.

✗ NOT APPROVED

Findings not resolved. Not added to ASL. Alternative supplier required.

Phase 4
Ongoing Monitoring and Re-qualification
10Incoming material testing programme
Risk-based incoming testing. For API suppliers, full identity testing on every lot per 21 CFR 211.84(d)(1). EU GMP Chapter 5.31 requires at least one identity test per container of active substance received.
⚠ Illustrative case pattern - COA-only release

A site with a strong, well-documented supplier qualification programme begins releasing incoming API lots based solely on the supplier's COA, without in-house identity testing, reasoning that the supplier has an excellent audit history. 21 CFR 211.84(d)(1) requires at least one identity test on each shipment of each component regardless of qualification status or audit history. A strong qualification programme upstream does not substitute for the identity testing requirement at the point of receipt.

21 CFR 211.84(d)  ·  EU GMP Chapter 5.31  ·  USP 1083
↓
11Performance monitoring and KPIs
Monitor COA compliance rate versus in-house testing, delivery performance, complaint and deviation rate, change notification timeliness, and audit finding closure rates. Reviewed quarterly for high-risk, annually for medium/low. Deteriorating performance triggers escalation.
✓ Best practice - escalate on trend, not just threshold breach

Track KPI trend direction, not only whether a KPI has crossed a hard threshold. A supplier whose COA compliance rate is declining quarter over quarter but still technically within tolerance is showing exactly the kind of early signal a monitoring programme exists to catch - treat the trend itself as the trigger for enhanced attention, not the eventual threshold breach.

EU GMP Chapter 8.14  ·  ICH Q10 Section 3.2.3  ·  USP 1083
↓
12Periodic re-qualification and re-audit
High risk: annually or bi-annually with on-site re-audit. Medium risk: every two to three years. Low risk: every three to five years. Also triggered by change notification, warning letter/import alert, significant quality event, or quality agreement expiry.
EU GMP Chapter 8.14 - regulatory basis

Supplier performance is expected to be continuously assessed rather than evaluated only at fixed re-qualification intervals, with significant events such as a new regulatory action against the supplier triggering re-qualification outside the standard cycle.

EU GMP Chapter 5.27  ·  ICH Q10 Section 3.2.3  ·  21 CFR 211.84
↓
Does re-qualification confirm continued GMP compliance?
YES → Renew on ASL NO → Suspend and investigate
↓
13Change notification management
Quality agreement must require notification of changes affecting quality, specifications or GMP status. Notifications assessed through change control. Changes to synthetic route, manufacturing site or specification may require Prior Approval Supplement before changed material can be used.
✓ Best practice - notification received is not notification assessed

A supplier change notification that is logged and filed, but never routed through formal change control for regulatory impact assessment, has not actually been managed - it has only been received. Build a hard gate that prevents any changed material from being accepted into inventory until the change control assessment is documented as complete, regardless of the supplier's assurance that quality is unaffected.

21 CFR 314.70  ·  EU GMP Chapter 7  ·  ICH Q12

GMP Supplier Audit Programme - What Inspectors Look For

Facility and manufacturing controls

Facility cleanliness and maintenance, equipment qualification status, pest control programme, environmental controls appropriate to the material, cross-contamination controls for multi-product facilities, cleaning validation for shared equipment.

Quality management system

Document control system, deviation and CAPA programme effectiveness including repeat deviation rates, change control system including how changes are communicated to customers, management review evidence.

Laboratory controls

OOS investigation programme, analytical method validation status, reference standard management, data integrity including audit trails and raw data accessibility, analyst training and qualification records.

Supply chain transparency

Whether the supplier is a manufacturer, broker or distributor. If a broker: who manufactures the material and is that manufacturer qualified? GDP compliance for storage and transport, temperature excursion management, chain of custody documentation.

⚠ Common Supplier Qualification Failures - FDA and EMA 2025-2026

Missing or incomplete quality agreementsThe most frequently cited deficiency. Quality agreements must exist for all contract manufacturers, contract testing laboratories and critical material suppliers. A general purchase agreement is not a quality agreement.
No on-site audit of API manufacturersEU GMP Chapter 5.27 requires active substance manufacturers be audited. Remote or desk-based assessment alone is not sufficient for API suppliers under EU GMP.
Relying on supplier COA without identity testing21 CFR 211.84(d)(1) requires at least one identity test on each shipment of each component. EU GMP Chapter 5.31 requires at least one identity test per container of active substance.
Change notifications not assessed through change controlNotifications received and acknowledged but not formally assessed. Material from a changed manufacturing process used without regulatory impact assessment.
ASL not maintained as a controlled documentNot kept current, not QA-controlled, or not used in purchasing - materials ordered from suppliers not on the ASL. The ASL is the primary evidence of programme operation.

Quality Agreement - Required Elements (EU GMP Chapter 7 and FDA 2016 Guidance)

Material specifications and release criteriaApproved specifications, testing requirements, COA format, retained sample requirements, shelf life and storage conditions.
Change control and notificationObligation to notify before implementing changes. Timelines typically 30 to 90 days for changes requiring regulatory assessment. Emergency change procedure.
Deviation and CAPA responsibilitiesWho investigates deviations in supplied materials. Investigation and CAPA timelines. Right to review supplier investigation reports.
Complaint handlingProcedure for raising complaints. Response timelines. Root cause investigation requirements. Replacement or credit procedure.
Audit rightsRight to on-site audits at agreed notice. Frequency by risk tier. Right to for-cause audits with short notice following a significant quality event.
GMP compliance maintenance and notificationObligation to maintain GMP certification and notify immediately of status changes, regulatory action, warning letters or import alerts.
Recall and field alert proceduresProcedure for supplier to notify of recalls or field alerts. Traceability requirements enabling identification of all manufacturer batches using affected supplier lots. Cooperation obligations during recall investigations.

Never do this

Use materials from an unqualified supplier in GMP manufacturing. Release API without identity testing. Accept a supplier COA as the sole basis for release without in-house testing. Allow change notifications to be filed without formal change control assessment. Use a supplier with an active warning letter or import alert without QA director-level risk acceptance.

EU API mandatory audit

EU GMP Chapter 5.27 requires active substance manufacturers be audited before approval and periodically thereafter, by the manufacturer or a qualified third party. A desk-based assessment without an on-site audit component does not satisfy Chapter 5.27 for API suppliers - one of the most frequently exploited gaps in EU supplier programmes.

21 CFR 211.84 - reduced testing conditions

Reduced incoming testing against supplier COA is permitted only after reliability of the supplier's analyses is established through appropriate validation at appropriate intervals - typically a documented comparative testing programme over two to three years, specific to the material, site and analytical methods in use.

Key regulations

21 CFR 211.84 - component testing and supplier approval. EU GMP Chapter 5.27 and 5.29 - starting material and API supplier qualification. EU GMP Chapter 7 - outsourced activities and quality agreements. ICH Q10 Section 3.2.3. USP 1083 - risk-based supplier qualification.
