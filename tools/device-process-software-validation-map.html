Validation - Evidence a Process or System Does What It Should

ISO 13485:2016 requires validation of production and service processes whose output cannot be fully verified, and of software used in production, the quality system, and monitoring and measurement. Part 820 incorporates ISO 13485 by reference under the QMSR. FDA guidance on computer software assurance describes a risk-based approach that focuses on the intended use of each software feature and scales the assurance activities to the process risk. This map follows the pathway from scoping and planning through risk-based approach, testing and record, and maintaining the validated state.

ISO 13485:2016 7.5.6
ISO 13485:2016 4.1.6
FDA CSA Guidance

Process Validation

Processes whose output cannot be fully verified by later monitoring or measurement are validated to show they consistently achieve planned results.

Basis: ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.5.6

Software Validation

Software used in production, the quality system, and monitoring and measurement is validated for its intended use, proportionate to the risk.

Basis: Clauses 4.1.6, 7.5.6, 7.6

Intended Use First

FDA guidance starts with the intended use of each feature, function, or operation, which drives the scope of validation.

Basis: FDA CSA Guidance

Risk-Based Assurance

Higher process risk calls for more rigor, often scripted testing. Lower process risk can use unscripted methods and lighter documentation.

Basis: FDA CSA Guidance

Rigor Scaling (Overview)

High Process Risk

Failure may cause a quality problem that foreseeably compromises safety. Rigor matches the device risk, often with scripted testing.

FDA CSA Guidance

Not High Process Risk

Rigor matches the process risk. Scenario testing, error guessing, or exploratory testing may suffice.

FDA CSA Guidance

Outside Scope

Software for general business operations or basic infrastructure generally falls outside validation, with the rationale recorded.

FDA CSA Guidance

When Validation Fails (Overview)

Do not release for use A process or system that does not meet acceptance criteria is not approved.
Assess product impact Check whether product already made or released could be affected, and route it through nonconforming product control if so.
Correct and retest Fix the cause, then repeat the affected activities and document the result.
Record the decision Document the issue, the resolution, and the conclusion before approval.

Validation Records (Overview)

Intended use and risk Intended use, the risk-based analysis, and the rationale for the approach.
Plan and activities The plan, the testing or process runs performed, and acceptance criteria.
Issues and resolution Issues found, their resolution, and any retesting.
Conclusion and approval The conclusion, who performed the work and when, and approval.

Validation Quality Elements

Documented Validation Procedure How validation is planned, carried out, and approved is defined.
Intended Use Defined Each process, feature, or function has a stated purpose.
Risk-Based Rigor Effort and documentation scale to the process risk.
Pre-set Acceptance Criteria Criteria are fixed before testing begins.
Qualified Equipment and Personnel Those involved are qualified for the work.
Objective Evidence Records show the system performs as intended.
Change Impact Assessment Every change is assessed for effect on the validated state.
Revalidation and Monitoring Revalidation and performance review keep the state current.

Never do this

Validate a system against the vendor demo instead of your own intended use. Write acceptance criteria after seeing the test results. Apply the same heavy documentation to every function whatever its risk. Update validated software with no impact assessment. Treat the validation as finished at go-live.

Intended use drives scope

FDA guidance starts from the intended use of each feature, function, or operation. Two features in the same system can carry different risk, so assess them separately and record the reasoning.

What FDA guidance does and does not say

FDA computer software assurance guidance is nonbinding. It describes a risk-based approach and cites ISO 13485 clauses 4.1.6, 7.5.6, and 7.6 as the source of validation requirements, without describing their content.

Key standards

ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.5.6 - validation of production processes. Clause 4.1.6 - software in the quality management system. Clause 7.6 - monitoring and measuring equipment. FDA computer software assurance guidance - risk-based approach for production and quality system software.
