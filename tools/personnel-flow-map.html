Personnel Flow - Controlling the Largest Contamination Source in the Room

Personnel shed skin cells, hair, and microorganisms continuously, making people the single largest source of particulate and microbial contamination in most classified manufacturing environments. Personnel flow describes the controlled, progressive path individuals take through gowning stages, airlocks, and classified areas, designed so that movement itself, not just training and vigilance, structurally limits contamination and cross-contamination risk. EU GMP Annex 1 2022 and Chapter 2 both expect this flow to be documented, qualified, and enforced through physical design as well as procedure.

Grade D Gowning

Basic protective clothing appropriate for the lowest classification supporting areas, typically the entry point into the gowning progression.

Typical use: Warehouse, low-risk support areas

Grade C Gowning

Enhanced protective clothing covering more of the body, worn in areas supporting less critical aseptic processing stages.

Typical use: Solution preparation, filtration

Grade B Gowning

Near-complete body coverage including sterile gloves and full head covering, worn in the background environment supporting Grade A operations.

Typical use: Background to aseptic filling

Grade A / Aseptic Gowning

Complete sterile gowning with no exposed skin, worn in the highest-risk zone directly supporting open product exposure during aseptic operations.

Typical use: Aseptic filling zone

Gowning Qualification Requirements

Initial Qualification

Documented demonstration of correct gowning technique through both visual assessment and microbial monitoring before independent area access is granted.

EU GMP Annex 1 2022

Periodic Requalification

Gowning qualification reassessed on a defined periodic schedule, confirming technique remains consistent over time rather than assuming initial qualification is permanent.

EU GMP Annex 1 2022

Level-Specific Authorization

Access authorization tied specifically to the classification levels an individual has qualified for, not granted as blanket facility-wide access.

EU GMP Chapter 2

Gowning Breach Response Steps

Step 1 - Exit the classified area immediately Leave the area through the correct exit path the moment a gowning breach, such as exposed skin or a torn glove, is identified.
Step 2 - Notify quality and the area supervisor Report the breach promptly so the potential impact on any in-progress operation can be assessed without delay.
Step 3 - Assess product and process impact Determine whether any product or process step in progress at the time of the breach requires investigation.
Step 4 - Correlate with environmental and personnel monitoring Review EM and personnel monitoring data from the same time period and location for any corroborating signal.
Step 5 - Document and evaluate retraining need Fully document the event and assess whether the individual requires gowning retraining or requalification before returning to the area.

Personnel Monitoring Requirements

Sampling at multiple gown locations Fingertips, forearms, chest, and other gown locations sampled to build a complete picture of gowning effectiveness across the body.
Sampling upon exit from Grade A/B areas Personnel monitoring performed as individuals exit the highest-risk classification areas, capturing the cumulative exposure of the session.
Individual trending over time Each individual's monitoring results trended over successive sessions to detect a gradual decline in gowning technique.
Correlation with environmental monitoring Personnel monitoring data reviewed alongside environmental monitoring results from the same session to build a complete contamination picture.

Personnel Movement Control Elements

Airlock Design and Pressure Cascade Airlocks designed with appropriate pressure differentials supporting directional airflow and preventing contamination migration between classification levels.
One-Way Personnel Flow Facility layout designed so personnel movement generally proceeds in one direction through increasing classification levels, minimizing backtracking.
Cross-Flow Prevention Physical design or procedural control preventing personnel gowned for one product or area from directly entering a different concurrent operation.
Product-Dedicated Personnel Personnel dedicated to a single product or process during a shift where cross-contamination risk between products is elevated.
Visitor and Contractor Control Visitors and contractors subject to the same gowning and access control requirements as regular personnel, with appropriate escort where required.
Hand Hygiene Stations Hand washing and disinfection stations positioned at every gowning transition point, stocked and functioning at all times.
Restricted Zone Signage Clear, consistent signage identifying classification level and access requirements at every entry point throughout the facility.
Training Frequency and Refresher Schedule Defined schedule for gowning refresher training, ensuring technique does not silently degrade over long periods of routine, unsupervised practice.

Never do this

Grant facility-wide access authorization regardless of which specific areas an individual actually qualified for. Allow personnel to move between concurrent product zones without regowning. Skip periodic gowning requalification once initial qualification is achieved. Continue an operation after a known gowning breach without impact assessment.

Personnel flow vs material flow

Personnel flow and material flow are designed around different risk logics. Personnel movement is governed by gowning level and classification access; material movement is governed by quality status and processing stage. Both must be addressed, since controlling one does not automatically control the other.

Gowning qualification vs training

Training conveys the correct gowning procedure and technique. Qualification is the documented, tested confirmation that an individual can actually perform that technique correctly and consistently, verified through both observation and microbial monitoring rather than assumed from training completion alone.
