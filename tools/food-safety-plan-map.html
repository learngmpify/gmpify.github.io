Food Safety Plan - Building a Written, Risk-Based Preventive Controls System

Under the FDA Food Safety Modernization Act, a registered food facility must prepare and implement a written food safety plan that begins with a hazard analysis and ends with a set of preventive controls matched to every hazard that requires one. The plan must be prepared, or its preparation overseen, by a Preventive Controls Qualified Individual (PCQI), and it must be validated, verified, and reanalyzed over time. This map details the pathway from hazard analysis through control development, documentation, and ongoing verification.

21 CFR Part 117 Subpart C  ·  FSMA Section 103  ·  PCQI
21 CFR 117 Subpart C
FSMA Section 103
PCQI Oversight

Process Controls

Procedures such as cooking, chilling, and acidification that reduce or prevent a hazard in the product during processing.

Basis: Parameters and values

Food Allergen Controls

Procedures that prevent allergen cross-contact and ensure the product is labeled correctly for its allergen content.

Basis: Cross-contact and labeling

Sanitation Controls

Procedures that keep the facility and food contact surfaces clean enough to prevent contamination from the environment.

Basis: Environment and hygiene

Supply-Chain Controls

Procedures that control hazards in raw materials and ingredients that are controlled by the supplier rather than the facility.

Basis: Supplier verification

Hazard Categories (Overview)

Biological Hazards

Bacteria, viruses, and parasites, such as Salmonella, Listeria monocytogenes, and norovirus, that can cause foodborne illness.

21 CFR 117.3

Chemical Hazards

Includes undeclared allergens, pesticide residues, natural toxins, food additives, and radiological hazards.

21 CFR 117.3

Physical Hazards

Foreign objects such as glass, metal fragments, and hard plastic that can cause injury when consumed.

21 CFR 117.3

Hazard Evaluation Criteria (Overview)

Severity of illness or injury How serious the harm would be if the hazard reached the consumer, from mild illness to hospitalization or death.
Probability of occurrence How likely the hazard is to occur in the absence of a preventive control, based on the process and the product.
Evidence considered Facility experience, illness data, scientific reports, and information from FDA and industry inform the evaluation.
Significance drives the control A hazard that is significant on severity and probability requires a preventive control, and the reasoning must be documented.

Key Food Safety Plan Outputs (Overview)

Written hazard analysis Documented identification and evaluation of every hazard, including the rationale where no control is required.
Written preventive controls Controls with their parameters, values, and management components documented for each significant hazard.
Implementation records Monitoring, corrective action, and verification records showing the plan is operating as written.
Reanalysis record Documented review of the plan, confirming it remains adequate or recording the changes made.

Food Safety Plan Quality Elements

PCQI Qualification Plan prepared or overseen by an individual with the training or experience the regulation requires.
Complete Hazard Identification Every biological, chemical, and physical hazard considered at every process step and for every ingredient.
Documented Hazard Evaluation Severity and probability reasoning recorded so the decision to control or not control a hazard can be defended.
Control Parameter Clarity Measurable parameters and values defined so operators know exactly what a controlled process looks like.
Monitoring Practicality Monitoring procedures, frequency, and responsible roles are realistic for the line and consistently performed.
Corrective Action Readiness Written responses ready for deviations, covering product disposition, root cause, and prevention of recurrence.
Validation Evidence Scientific or technical evidence retained showing each control can achieve the hazard reduction claimed.
Reanalysis Discipline Three-year review scheduled, with defined triggers for earlier reanalysis after changes or failures.

Never do this

Write the plan without a qualified individual's preparation or oversight. Copy a generic hazard analysis without considering your own product and process. Rely on a preventive control that has never been validated. Wait for the three-year cycle to reanalyze after a significant process change or control failure.

Food safety plan vs HACCP plan

A HACCP plan is built on seven principles, with critical control points and critical limits. A FSMA food safety plan is broader, adding allergen, sanitation, and supply-chain controls, a written recall plan, and PCQI oversight, and it does not require every control to be a critical control point.

Corrections vs corrective actions

A correction is a timely step to address a minor, isolated issue that does not directly affect product safety, such as cleaning a surface. A corrective action responds to a preventive control not being implemented or a deviation from a parameter, and requires product evaluation and root cause review.

Key regulations

21 CFR 117 Subpart C - hazard analysis and risk-based preventive controls. FSMA Section 103 - statutory basis for preventive controls. 21 CFR 117.126 to 117.170 - requirements for the plan, controls, validation, verification, and reanalysis. 21 CFR 117.180 - PCQI requirements.
