Tech Transfer - Moving Product and Process Knowledge Between Sites

A technology transfer moves product and process knowledge from a sending unit, such as development or an existing manufacturing site, to a receiving unit responsible for future commercial production. Success is measured not by completing the transfer activities but by the receiving site's demonstrated ability to reliably reproduce the product to the same quality standard, supported by a documented knowledge base under ICH Q10's Pharmaceutical Quality System. This map details the pathway from planning through knowledge transfer, validation, and ongoing verification.

Transfer Readiness Criteria (Overview)

Facility and equipment equivalency Receiving site facilities and equipment demonstrated equivalent or adequately bridged to the sending site's requirements.
Analytical method transferability Test methods demonstrated to perform equivalently in the receiving laboratory before release testing relies on them.
Personnel training and competency Receiving site personnel trained and demonstrated competent in the transferred process before validation begins.
Readiness drives the go/no-go decision Validation batches proceed only once every readiness criterion has been formally confirmed and documented.

Key Transfer Outputs (Overview)

Technology transfer report Comprehensive record documenting the complete transfer, from gap assessment through validation comparability.
Comparability assessment Formal conclusion on whether the receiving site's product is equivalent to the sending site's historical product.
Process validation batch data Validation batch results demonstrating the receiving site can reliably reproduce the product at commercial scale.
Quality and regulatory approval Formal sign-off enabling commercial production and distribution from the receiving site.

Tech Transfer Quality Elements

Gap Assessment Thoroughness Facility, equipment, and capability gaps identified comprehensively before the transfer plan is finalized.
Knowledge Documentation Completeness Product and process knowledge transferred in complete, well-organized documentation rather than informal communication.
Analytical Method Transfer Success Methods demonstrated equivalent at the receiving laboratory through a formal, predefined transfer protocol.
Equipment Qualification Rigor New or bridged equipment qualified to the same standard as the sending site before validation batches proceed.
Cross-Site Communication Sending and receiving site teams maintain active, structured communication throughout the transfer.
Validation Batch Comparability Receiving site validation results formally compared and shown equivalent to the sending site's historical performance.
Regulatory Filing Alignment Regulatory filings updated to reflect the new manufacturing site ahead of or alongside commercial production.
Post-Transfer Monitoring Continuity Continued process verification maintained after transfer to confirm the process remains in a validated state.

Never do this

Begin validation batches before the gap assessment and readiness criteria are formally closed. Rely on informal knowledge transfer instead of complete documentation. Treat analytical method transfer as automatic without a formal equivalency study. Consider the transfer complete without a documented comparability conclusion.

Tech transfer vs process validation

Technology transfer is the broader activity of moving product and process knowledge and capability to a new site. Process validation is one component within it, the formal demonstration that the receiving site's process reliably produces conforming product, but transfer also includes knowledge, methods, and equipment readiness.

Site-to-site vs development transfer

A development-to-manufacturing transfer establishes a commercial process for the first time, often with less mature process knowledge. A site-to-site transfer moves an already validated commercial process, so the emphasis shifts toward demonstrating equivalency rather than establishing the process itself.

Key regulations

ICH Q10 - pharmaceutical quality system expectations spanning the product lifecycle. WHO TRS 961 Annex 7 - dedicated technology transfer guidance. EU GMP Chapter 7 - outsourced activities and contract manufacturing oversight expectations.
