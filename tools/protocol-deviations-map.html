Protocol Deviations - Managing Departures From the Approved Plan

A protocol deviation is any departure from the approved protocol or GCP requirements, whether accidental or intentional. ICH E6(R2) requires that deviations be documented, and that their impact on subject safety, rights, and data reliability be assessed. EU CTR 536/2014 introduces the distinct concept of a serious breach, a deviation likely to affect subject safety, rights, or the reliability of trial results, which carries its own expedited notification requirement. This map details the pathway from identification through classification, reporting, and corrective action.

Non-Important Deviation

Limited impact on subject safety, rights, or data reliability. Logged and reported through routine channels.

Reporting: Routine / periodic

Important Deviation

Significant potential effect on subject safety, rights, or the reliability of trial data. Requires expedited handling.

Reporting: Sponsor and IRB, expedited

Serious Breach

EU CTR concept: a breach likely to substantially affect subject safety, rights, or result reliability.

Reporting: Competent authority, 7 days

Prospective Waiver

A planned, pre-approved departure from a protocol requirement for a specific subject, distinct from an unplanned deviation.

Reporting: Prior sponsor/IRB approval

Common Root Cause Categories (Overview)

Visit window non-compliance Subject visit conducted outside the protocol-specified window, often driven by subject availability or site scheduling constraints.
Eligibility criteria misapplication Subject enrolled despite not fully meeting inclusion or exclusion criteria, typically traced to an incomplete eligibility review.
Procedural or dosing error Study procedure performed incorrectly or investigational product dosed outside the protocol-specified regimen.
Informed consent process gaps Consent obtained using an outdated version of the form, or without all protocol-required elements documented.

Reporting Timelines (Overview)

Important deviation to sponsor Reported within the timeline specified by the protocol or monitoring plan, typically within a small number of business days.
Important deviation to IRB / EC Reported per the overseeing body's own reporting requirements, which may differ from the sponsor timeline.
Serious breach to competent authority Notified within 7 days of the sponsor becoming aware, per EU CTR 536/2014, distinct from routine deviation reporting.
Non-important deviations Summarized in periodic reports rather than reported individually and immediately.

Protocol Deviation Quality Elements

Deviation Log Maintenance Site-level log capturing every deviation with dates of occurrence and discovery, description, and classification.
Investigator Review and Sign-Off Every deviation reviewed and acknowledged by the investigator, consistent with their responsibility for site conduct.
Classification Consistency Important versus non-important classification applied consistently across sites using the same defined criteria.
Root Cause Documentation Underlying cause investigated and documented for every important deviation, not just the deviation's description.
Sponsor Notification Timeliness Important deviations and serious breaches reported within the applicable regulatory or protocol-defined timeline.
IRB / EC Reporting Deviations reported to the overseeing ethics body per its own specific requirements and timeline.
CAPA Implementation Corrective action addressing the immediate deviation and preventive action addressing its underlying cause, both documented.
Deviation Trending Deviation data reviewed across sites and time to detect systemic issues in site performance or protocol design.

Never do this

Classify a deviation without a documented impact assessment. Treat a serious breach as a routine deviation and miss the 7-day competent authority notification. Close a deviation without addressing its root cause. Allow the same deviation type to recur at a site without escalation.

Deviation vs violation

A protocol deviation is typically unintentional and does not necessarily indicate a systemic problem. A protocol violation reflects a willful or repeated departure from the protocol, often carrying more serious consequences for the site's continued participation.

Prospective waiver vs deviation

A prospective waiver is a planned, pre-approved departure from a specific protocol requirement, requested and approved before it occurs. An unplanned deviation is identified only after it has already happened and follows the standard deviation reporting pathway.

Key regulations

ICH E6(R2) Section 4.5 - investigator compliance with the protocol and deviation handling. EU CTR 536/2014 Art. 52 - serious breach definition and notification. 21 CFR 312.66 - investigator's obligation to conduct the trial per the protocol.
