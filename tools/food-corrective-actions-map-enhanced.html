Corrective Actions and Corrections - Responding When a Preventive Control Fails

When a preventive control is not properly implemented, the facility must act. Under 21 CFR 117.150, a corrective action identifies and corrects the problem, reduces the likelihood of recurrence, evaluates all affected food for safety, and keeps affected food from commerce if it cannot be shown to be safe and properly labeled. A correction is a timely fix for a minor, isolated problem that does not directly affect product safety. This map follows the pathway from detection through classification, evaluation, documentation, and reanalysis.

FSMA Section 103
Affected Food

Corrective Action

Action taken when a preventive control is not properly implemented, to correct the problem, prevent recurrence, and evaluate affected food.

Basis: 21 CFR 117.150(a)

Correction

A timely fix for a minor and isolated problem that does not directly impact product safety, or for certain allergen and sanitation conditions.

Basis: 21 CFR 117.150(c)

Unanticipated Problem

A control with no corrective action procedure, a control or plan found ineffective, or a records review that finds failures.

Basis: 21 CFR 117.150(b)

Affected Food

All food potentially impacted by the problem, evaluated for safety and held from commerce if it cannot be shown safe and properly labeled.

Triggers for Corrective Action (Overview)

Control Not Properly Implemented

A deviation from a parameter, a missed check, or any case where the preventive control did not operate as the plan requires.

21 CFR 117.150(a)

Positive Test Results

A pathogen or indicator organism found in a ready-to-eat product by product testing, or in the environment by environmental monitoring.

21 CFR 117.150(a)(1)

Ineffective Control or Review Findings

A control or the plan as a whole found ineffective, or a records review finding incomplete records, activities not performed, or inappropriate decisions.

21 CFR 117.150(b)(1)

Corrective Action Content (Overview)

Identify and correct the problem Appropriate action is taken to find and correct the problem with the preventive control.
Reduce the likelihood of recurrence When necessary, action is taken so that the problem is less likely to happen again.
Evaluate all affected food for safety Every unit that may have been impacted is evaluated, not only the unit that tested out of range.
Keep affected food from commerce Food is not released if the facility cannot ensure it is not adulterated or misbranded.

Key Corrective Action Outputs (Overview)

Written corrective action procedures Procedures for each preventive control, including for positive pathogen or indicator results.
Corrective action records The problem, the affected food, the evaluation, the decision, and the actions taken to correct and prevent.
Correction records Records of timely fixes, where appropriate, so the pattern of minor issues is visible.
Reanalysis record Documentation of whether the food safety plan needed to change after a failure.

Corrective Action Quality Elements

Prompt Detection Problems found through monitoring and verification close to when they occur, not at the next audit.
Rapid Containment Affected food held and the process controlled as soon as a problem is suspected.
Clear Classification Defined criteria for a correction versus a corrective action, applied consistently.
Root Cause Understanding The real reason the control failed is understood, not just the visible symptom.
Complete Scope All food that may have been affected is identified, by time, line, and lot.
Sound Product Evaluation Safety decisions made from data and competent judgment and written down with reasoning.
Effective Prevention Actions that reduce the chance of repeat failures and are confirmed to work.
Documentation and Review Complete records, reviewed on time, that show what happened and why decisions were made.

Regulatory Text - Source Provisions

21 CFR 117.150(a)(2) (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

Corrective action procedures must describe the steps to ensure that appropriate action is taken to identify and correct the problem with the preventive control, that when necessary action is taken to reduce the likelihood that the problem will recur, that all affected food is evaluated for safety, and that affected food is prevented from entering commerce if the facility cannot ensure it is not adulterated under section 402 or misbranded under section 403(w) of the FD&C Act.

21 CFR 117.150(b) (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

When a preventive control is not properly implemented and no corrective action procedure exists, when a control or the food safety plan as a whole is found ineffective, or when a records review finds incomplete records, activities not performed as required, or inappropriate corrective action decisions, the facility must take corrective action and, when appropriate, reanalyze the food safety plan to determine whether it must be modified.

21 CFR 117.150(c) (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

The corrective action requirements do not apply if the facility acts in a timely manner to identify and correct conditions and practices inconsistent with certain food allergen and sanitation controls, or acts in a timely manner to correct a minor and isolated problem that does not directly impact product safety.

Common Inspection Findings - Corrective Actions and Corrections

Illustrative of recurring themes seen across published FDA Form 483s and warning letters. Not tied to a single specific citation.
No written corrective action procedures for a preventive control A control exists in the plan but no procedure describes what to do when it is not properly implemented.
Deviations recorded but affected food not evaluated A record shows the process was adjusted, but no one evaluated or documented what happened to the food made during the deviation.
Corrections used for problems that affect product safety A temperature or allergen failure is closed as a simple fix, with no corrective action or product evaluation.
No reanalysis after repeated or serious failures The same control fails again and again without anyone asking whether the plan, or the control, is still adequate.

Best Practice Guidance

Write the decision tree in advance Give supervisors a short flow that says when to hold, who to call, and how to classify a problem as a correction or a corrective action.
Define affected food by time window Use the last verified good check and the next verified good check to set the scope of food that must be evaluated.
Train supervisors on classification The decision between a correction and a corrective action is made on the floor, so the people making it need practice.
Trend corrective actions Review actions together over time, so repeat causes drive reanalysis and plan changes.

Never do this

Call a real deviation a correction to avoid the paperwork. Release product before evaluating it. Fix the symptom and skip the cause. Close a corrective action without checking it worked. Treat repeated corrections of the same problem as routine instead of a signal to reanalyze.

Correction vs corrective action

A correction is a timely fix for a minor, isolated problem that does not directly impact product safety, or for conditions inconsistent with certain allergen or sanitation controls. A corrective action is required when a preventive control is not properly implemented, and it includes evaluating affected food.

Unanticipated problems

If a control is not implemented and has no corrective action procedure, if a control or the plan is found ineffective, or if a records review finds failures, the facility must take corrective action and, when appropriate, reanalyze the plan.
