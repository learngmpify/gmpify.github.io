Clinical Trial Workflow - The GCP Framework for Human Subject Research

A clinical trial is a highly structured research activity governed by Good Clinical Practice, the international ethical and scientific quality standard for designing, conducting, recording, and reporting trials that involve human subjects. ICH E6(R2) sets out the framework connecting protocol design, informed consent, investigator obligations, sponsor oversight, and data integrity into a single system whose purpose is protecting subject rights and safety while ensuring the resulting data is credible. This workflow map traces the trial lifecycle from startup through closeout, with the specific detail of SAE reporting, monitoring visits, TMF management, and protocol deviations each covered in their own dedicated map.

Phase I

First-in-human studies primarily assessing safety, tolerability, and dosing in a small number of subjects or healthy volunteers.

Focus: Safety, dosing, PK/PD

Phase II

Studies exploring preliminary efficacy and further characterizing side effects in a larger, more targeted patient population.

Focus: Efficacy signal, dose-finding

Phase III

Large-scale, pivotal confirmatory studies establishing efficacy and safety in a population representative of intended real-world use.

Focus: Confirmatory efficacy and safety

Phase IV

Post-marketing studies conducted after approval, monitoring long-term safety and effectiveness in broader, real-world use.

Focus: Post-marketing surveillance

Core GCP Roles and Responsibilities

Sponsor

Responsible for overall trial design, quality oversight, safety reporting, and ensuring the trial is conducted in compliance with the protocol and GCP.

ICH E6(R2)

Investigator

Responsible for conducting the trial at the site level, protecting subject rights and safety, and ensuring data accuracy and protocol adherence.

ICH E6(R2)

IRB / Ethics Committee

Responsible for independent review and ongoing oversight of the trial's ethical acceptability and protection of subject welfare.

Safety Event Escalation Steps (Overview)

Step 1 - Identify and assess the event Investigator identifies the adverse event and makes an initial assessment of its seriousness and relationship to the investigational product.
Step 2 - Notify the sponsor per required timeline Serious adverse events reported to the sponsor within the timeline specified by the protocol and applicable regulation.
Step 3 - Sponsor evaluates for expedited reporting Sponsor evaluates the event against the investigator's brochure to determine whether expedited regulatory reporting is required.
Step 4 - Notify the IRB and other sites as applicable Relevant safety information communicated to the IRB and, where applicable, to other participating sites.

Clinical Trial Quality Elements

Informed Consent Process Documented process ensuring subjects understand the trial's risks and benefits and voluntarily agree to participate before any study procedure begins.
Investigational Product Accountability Complete, traceable record of investigational product receipt, dispensing, and return maintained throughout the trial.
Source Data Verification Case report form data verified against original source documents to confirm accuracy and completeness.
Risk-Based Monitoring Monitoring intensity and focus scaled to the specific risks of the trial, rather than applying uniform monitoring regardless of risk profile.
Data Management Plan Defined plan for data collection, cleaning, and query resolution, supporting a reliable and traceable trial database.
Protocol Deviation Management System for identifying, documenting, and assessing the impact of any departure from the approved protocol.
Safety Reporting Timelines Defined timelines for reporting adverse events and serious adverse events to the sponsor, IRB, and regulatory authorities.
Trial Master File Complete, organized collection of essential documents demonstrating the trial was conducted in compliance with GCP and applicable regulation.

Regulatory Text - Source Provisions

ICH E6(R2), Section 2.3

The rights, safety, and well-being of the trial subjects are the most important considerations and should prevail over interests of science and society.

ICH E6(R2), Section 2.10

All clinical trial information should be recorded, handled, and stored in a way that allows its accurate reporting, interpretation, and verification.

21 CFR 312.60 (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

An investigator is responsible for ensuring that an investigation is conducted according to the signed investigator statement, the investigational plan, and applicable regulations, for protecting the rights, safety, and welfare of subjects, and for the control of drugs under investigation.

Common Inspection Findings - Clinical Trial Conduct

Illustrative of recurring themes seen across published FDA 483s and warning letters. Not tied to a single specific citation.
Informed consent obtained after study procedures began Consent documentation dated after screening tests or other protocol procedures had already occurred, undermining the voluntariness of the subject's agreement.
Protocol deviations not documented or assessed Departures from the approved protocol identified during monitoring or audit with no corresponding deviation record or impact assessment on file.
Investigational product accountability gaps Dispensing and return records that do not reconcile, or missing documentation of product storage conditions at the site.
Delegation of duties not documented Site staff performing trial-related tasks without a corresponding entry on the delegation of authority log signed by the investigator.

Best Practice Guidance

Build the monitoring plan around trial risk Scale monitoring frequency and depth to the specific risks of the protocol and investigational product rather than applying a fixed visit schedule to every trial.
Keep the delegation log current Update the delegation of authority log the moment site staff or responsibilities change, so every task performed maps back to a documented, qualified individual.
Close monitoring findings before the next visit Track corrective actions from each monitoring visit to closure, and confirm resolution is verified at the following visit rather than carried forward indefinitely.
Treat the TMF as a living file, not a closeout task Filing essential documents as the trial proceeds, rather than reconstructing the file at closeout, keeps the trial inspection-ready at any point in its lifecycle.

Never do this

Enroll a subject before documented informed consent is obtained. Administer investigational product without a complete, traceable accountability record. GCP vs GMP

Good Manufacturing Practice governs how the investigational product itself is manufactured, tested, and released. Good Clinical Practice governs how the trial using that product is designed, conducted, and reported. Both apply to the same investigational product at different points in its journey to and through a clinical trial.

Sponsor vs CRO responsibilities

A sponsor may delegate specific trial-related duties, such as monitoring or data management, to a Contract Research Organization. Regulatory responsibility for trial oversight and compliance remains with the sponsor regardless of delegation, making sponsor oversight of the CRO's performance an ongoing obligation.
