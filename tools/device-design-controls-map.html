Design Controls - Turning User Needs Into a Safe, Effective, and Manufacturable Device

Since the Quality Management System Regulation took effect on 2 February 2026, 21 CFR Part 820 incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference, and design and development requirements come from clause 7.3. Under 21 CFR 820.10(c) they apply to all Class II and Class III devices and to certain listed Class I devices. This map follows the pathway from planning and design inputs through outputs, review, verification, validation, transfer, change control, and the design and development file.

21 CFR 820.10(c)  ·  820.7  ·  ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.3.1 to 7.3.10
21 CFR 820.10(c)
ISO 13485:2016 7.3
ISO 13485:2016 7.1

Design Input

Functional, performance, usability, and safety requirements tied to intended use, including regulatory and standards requirements and risk management outputs.

Basis: Clause 7.3.3

Design Output

Results of design that meet the inputs and give information for purchasing, production, and servicing, with acceptance criteria.

Basis: Clause 7.3.4

Verification

Confirms the outputs meet the inputs. Did we make the device correctly?

Basis: Clause 7.3.6

Validation

Confirms the device meets the needs of its intended use. Did we make the correct device?

Basis: Clause 7.3.7

Applicability (Overview)

Class II and Class III Devices

Design and development requirements apply to all manufacturers of Class II and Class III devices.

21 CFR 820.10(c)

Listed Class I Devices

Also apply to certain Class I devices, including computer software automated devices, tracheobronchial suction catheters, non-powdered surgeon gloves, protective restraints, manual radionuclide applicator systems, and radionuclide teletherapy sources.

21 CFR 820.10(c)

When to Start

Recommended once research ends or after feasibility. Required before clinical evaluations such as IDE studies and before submissions such as 510(k), De Novo, PMA, or HDE.

When Verification or Validation Fails (Overview)

Record the failure Document what was tested, what the result was, and which requirement or acceptance criterion was not met.
Assess the impact Evaluate effects on design inputs, outputs, and the risk management file before deciding on action.
Change under control Make the change through the design change process, reviewed and approved before it is implemented.
Repeat the affected work Re-verify or re-validate what the change affects and update the design and development file.

Design and Development File (Overview)

Plan and inputs The design and development plan and the recorded design inputs.
Outputs and reviews Design outputs, review results, and the actions that followed.
Verification and validation Plans, methods, acceptance criteria, results, and clinical evaluation where required.
Transfer and changes Transfer records and every design change with its review and approval.

Design Control Quality Elements

Design and Development Plan Stages, reviews, responsibilities, resources, and traceability method defined before work starts.
Complete Design Inputs Inputs are complete, unambiguous, verifiable or validatable, and free of conflict.
Traceability Each input traces to outputs, verification, and validation.
Risk Management Linkage Risk management outputs drive inputs, and design decisions update the risk file.
Design Review Records Reviews held at suitable stages, with results and actions recorded and closed.
Verification and Validation Plans Documented plans with methods, acceptance criteria, and statistical techniques.
Transfer Readiness Outputs confirmed suitable for manufacturing before they become production specifications.
Change and File Control Changes approved before implementation, with the design file kept current.

Never do this

Start designing before the inputs are recorded and reviewed. Verify against the outputs instead of the inputs. Validate on a prototype that does not represent the production device. Implement a design change before it is reviewed and approved.

Verification vs validation

Verification confirms the design outputs meet the design inputs: did we make the device correctly. Validation confirms the device meets the needs of its intended use: did we make the correct device. Both need documented plans, and validation is completed before release.

The design and development file

Keep a file for each device type or family that includes or references the records showing conformity with the design requirements, including records of design changes. Under the QMSR this file replaces the term design history file used in the former regulation.

Key regulations

21 CFR 820.10(c) - applicability of design and development. 21 CFR 820.7 - incorporation of ISO 13485:2016 by reference. ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.3 - design and development. Clause 7.1 - risk management in product realization.
