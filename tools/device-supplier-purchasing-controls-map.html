Supplier Controls - Approval Is the Start, Not the End

A device is only as reliable as what goes into it. Under the Quality Management System Regulation, the former purchasing controls section, 21 CFR 820.50, is replaced by ISO 13485:2016 clause 7.4, which Part 820 incorporates by reference. Clause 7.4 covers the purchasing process and supplier evaluation, purchasing information, and verification of purchased product. This map follows the pathway from defining requirements through supplier selection, purchasing and verification, and ongoing monitoring.

Purchasing Process

A documented procedure for evaluating, selecting, monitoring, and re-evaluating suppliers, with controls proportionate to the effect on the device and the risk.

Basis: Clause 7.4.1

Purchasing Information

Purchasing information describes the product to be purchased, including specifications and requirements for acceptance, and is approved before it is given to the supplier.

Basis: Clause 7.4.2

Verification of Purchased Product

Inspection or other activities confirm purchased product meets purchasing requirements, with the extent based on the supplier evaluation and the risk.

Basis: Clause 7.4.3

Supplier Change Notification

Written agreements require suppliers to tell the manufacturer of changes to purchased product before they are implemented.

Basis: Clause 7.4.2

Control Scaling (Overview)

Higher Effect on the Device

Critical components, sterile barrier materials, and outsourced processes call for deeper evaluation, such as audits, and tighter verification.

ISO 13485:2016 7.4.1, 7.4.3

Moderate Effect

Controls such as questionnaires, certificates, and risk-based inspection or testing of received product.

ISO 13485:2016 7.4.1, 7.4.3

Lower Effect

Lighter controls, with the rationale for the level of control recorded.

ISO 13485:2016 7.4.1

When a Supplier Falls Short (Overview)

Contain the product Identify and control affected purchased product and handle it as nonconforming product.
Raise the issue Notify the supplier, request investigation, and agree corrective action proportionate to the risk.
Change the controls Increase verification, add audits, or restrict the scope of approval while the problem is open.
Decide on the supplier Re-evaluate and, where needed, qualify an alternate source and record the decision.

Supplier Records (Overview)

Evaluation and approval Criteria, evaluation results, and the approved scope for each supplier.
Purchasing information Specifications, quality agreements, and approvals of what is given to the supplier.
Verification Results of inspection or other activities on received product.
Monitoring and re-evaluation Performance data, supplier actions, and re-evaluation results.

Supplier Control Quality Elements

Documented Procedure Evaluation, selection, monitoring, and re-evaluation are defined.
Risk-Based Controls The level of control reflects the effect on the device and the risk.
Clear Requirements Purchasing information describes the product and acceptance criteria.
Written Agreements Suppliers agree to notify changes before implementation.
Defined Evaluation Criteria Suppliers are chosen against stated criteria, and the result is recorded.
Verification of Received Product Acceptance activities match the risk and the supplier evaluation.
Ongoing Monitoring Performance is tracked and used, not just filed.
Re-evaluation and Records Suppliers are re-evaluated and every decision is recorded.

Never do this

Approve a supplier once and never review it again. Apply the same controls to every supplier regardless of risk. Let a supplier change a material or process without telling you. Issue purchase orders without approved requirements. Keep evaluation results that do not record the decision.

Control follows risk

ISO 13485 requires the level of control over suppliers and purchased product to be proportionate to the effect on the finished device and the associated risk. Record why each supplier received the level of control it did.

What changed under the QMSR

The former purchasing controls section, 21 CFR 820.50, is replaced by ISO 13485 clause 7.4. Expectations for ongoing monitoring and re-evaluation are more explicit in the ISO text. Companies should confirm their procedures against the clause wording.

Key standards

ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.4.1 - purchasing process. Clause 7.4.2 - purchasing information. Clause 7.4.3 - verification of purchased product. Clause 4.1.5 - outsourced processes. 21 CFR 820.10(a) and 820.7 - QMS requirement and incorporation of ISO 13485.
