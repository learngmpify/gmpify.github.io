Complaints and Serious Adverse Event Reporting - Receiving, Investigating, and Reporting What Comes Back From the Market

Dietary supplement manufacturers must follow written procedures to review every product complaint, investigate those that suggest a specification failure or other Part 111 problem, and keep a record of each complaint. Separately, the responsible person named on the label must report serious adverse events to FDA on the MedWatch form within 15 business days, with a copy of the label, and must send new medical information within the same time. This map details the pathway from receipt through review, investigation, reporting, response, and record keeping.

FD&C Act Section 761
MedWatch Report

Product Complaint

Any complaint received about a dietary supplement. Each is reviewed, and those suggesting a possible failure are investigated.

Basis: Subpart O

Serious Adverse Event

An adverse event with death, a life-threatening experience, hospitalization, lasting disability, birth defect, or need for medical or surgical intervention.

Basis: FD&C Act Section 761

Responsible Person

The manufacturer, packer, or distributor whose name is on the label. This person submits the serious adverse event report to FDA.

Basis: Named on the label

Quality Control

Quality control decides whether to investigate a complaint and approves the findings and follow-up actions.

Basis: 111.560(b)

Report Types (Overview)

Product Complaint

Reviewed by a qualified person. Investigated when it suggests a possible specification failure or other Part 111 problem.

21 CFR 111.560

Serious Adverse Event

Reported to FDA with the label copy within 15 business days of receipt.

FD&C Act Section 761

Other Adverse Events

Not submitted under Section 761, but records of adverse event reports received are retained for 6 years.

FD&C Act Section 761

Serious Adverse Event Criteria (Overview)

Death or life-threatening event The event results in death or a life-threatening experience.
Hospitalization The event results in inpatient hospitalization.
Lasting harm The event results in a persistent or significant disability or incapacity, or a congenital anomaly or birth defect.
Intervention needed Based on reasonable medical judgment, the event requires medical or surgical intervention to prevent one of the outcomes above.

Key Outputs (Overview)

Complaint record A written record of each complaint with the required details, made at the time.
Investigation record Findings and follow-up actions, approved by quality control, covering all relevant batches.
MedWatch submission Report with label copy, plus any follow-up medical information, sent on time.
Retained records Complaint records under Subpart P and adverse event records kept for 6 years.

Complaint and Adverse Event Quality Elements

Label Contact Information Consumers can reach the responsible person through the address or phone number on the label.
Single Intake Process Every channel, including calls, email, and social media, feeds the same log.
Qualified Reviewer A qualified person reviews each complaint and recognizes possible failures and adverse events.
QC Investigation Authority Quality control decides on investigation and approves findings and follow-up.
Batch Linkage Complaints are linked to the batch, so all relevant batches and records can be reviewed.
Deadline Tracking The 15 business day clock is tracked from receipt, with a named owner.
Contemporaneous Documentation Each task is documented at the time it is performed, with the required complaint details.
Trend Review Complaint and adverse event trends are reviewed and drive corrective action.

Never do this

Treat a complaint as a customer service matter only. Wait for confirmation of cause before reporting a possible serious adverse event. Investigate only the complained-of unit and ignore other batches. Leave a complaint without a written record of the reply and follow-up.

Complaint vs adverse event

A product complaint is any complaint about the product and is handled under Part 111 Subpart O. A serious adverse event is a health outcome meeting the statutory definition and is reported to FDA under Section 761. One report can be both, and then both processes apply.

When the 15 days start

The 15 business day deadline runs from receipt of the report through the address or phone number on the label. The report does not wait for the investigation or for proof the product caused the event, and sending it is not an admission of causation.

Key regulations

21 CFR 111.553 - written procedures. 111.560 - review and investigation of complaints. 111.570 - complaint records. FD&C Act Section 761 (21 USC 379aa-1) - serious adverse event reporting and records.
