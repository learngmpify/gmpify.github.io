Medical Device Risk Management - From Hazard Identification to Post-Production Feedback

ISO 13485:2016 clause 7.1, which the QMSR incorporates, requires documented risk management processes and records of risk management activities across product realization. ISO 14971:2019 is the standard most manufacturers use to do it. FDA has stated that it does not incorporate ISO 14971 into the regulation. This map follows the ISO 14971 pathway from the plan and risk analysis through evaluation, control, overall residual risk, review, and production and post-production activities.

Risk Management Plan

Established before risk analysis begins. Sets scope, responsibilities, risk acceptability criteria, the overall residual risk method, and post-production activities.

Basis: Clause 4.4

Risk Analysis

Intended use and reasonably foreseeable misuse, safety characteristics, hazards and hazardous situations, and risk estimation.

Basis: Clause 5

Risk Control

Control options in priority order, implementation, verification of effectiveness, residual risk, benefit-risk analysis, and new risks from the controls.

Basis: Clause 7

Overall Residual Risk

The combined effect of all residual risks evaluated against the benefits of the intended use.

Basis: Clause 8

Risk Control Priority (Overview)

1. Inherently Safe Design and Manufacture

Eliminate or reduce the hazard through the design itself. This is the first option to consider.

ISO 14971:2019 7.1

2. Protective Measures

Add protection in the device or the manufacturing process, such as guards, alarms, or interlocks, where design cannot remove the hazard.

ISO 14971:2019 7.1

3. Information for Safety and Training

Warnings, instructions, and user training. The last line of defence, not the first.

ISO 14971:2019 7.1

When Risk Is Not Acceptable (Overview)

Control the risk Select and implement control measures in the priority order and verify they are effective.
Re-evaluate the residual risk Apply the same acceptability criteria to the risk that remains after the controls.
Check what the control changed Look for new hazards or changed risk estimates introduced by the control measure.
Benefit-risk analysis Where further control is not practicable, gather data to determine whether the benefits outweigh the residual risk, and record the result.

Post-Production Information (Overview)

What to collect Production, user, service, supply chain, public, and state of the art information.
What to look for New hazards, a risk estimate or overall residual risk that is no longer acceptable, or a change in the state of the art.
What to do Review the risk file, decide whether reassessment is needed, and evaluate the impact on existing controls.
Where it feeds Updates to the risk management file and, where relevant, design changes through change control.

Risk Management Quality Elements

Policy and Acceptability Criteria Top management defines the policy, and the plan states clear criteria for acceptable risk.
Plan Before Analysis The risk management plan exists before risk analysis begins.
Foreseeable Misuse Considered Reasonably foreseeable misuse is analyzed, not only intended use.
Hazard to Residual Risk Traceability Each hazard traces through analysis, evaluation, controls, verification, and residual risk.
Control Priority Followed Design and protective measures are considered before warnings and training.
Effectiveness Verified Controls are verified as implemented and shown to be effective.
Overall Residual Risk Evaluated The combined residual risk is evaluated, not only each hazard on its own.
Post-Production Feedback Loop Production and post-market information is collected, reviewed, and fed into the risk file.

Regulatory Text - Source Provisions

ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.1, as incorporated by 21 CFR 820.10(a) (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

The organization must document processes for risk management in product realization and maintain records of the risk management activities. Risk management is part of design and development.

ISO 14971:2019 Clauses 4.5 and 7.1 (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

The risk management file must trace each identified hazard to its risk analysis, risk evaluation, the implementation and verification of its risk control measures, and the evaluation of its residual risk. Risk control options are considered in order of priority: inherently safe design and manufacture, protective measures, then information for safety.

ISO 14971:2019 Clauses 8 and 10 (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

The overall residual risk is evaluated, and significant residual risks are disclosed to users where it is acceptable. Production and post-production information is actively collected and reviewed, and the findings are used to review and, where needed, update the risk management file.

Common Findings - Medical Device Risk Management

Illustrative of recurring themes seen in device quality system inspections and audits. Not tied to a single specific citation. Note that FDA has stated it does not incorporate ISO 14971 into the regulation, so findings are typically framed under the QMSR risk management requirement.
Risk management file incomplete or not traceable Hazards that cannot be traced through analysis, control, verification, and residual risk evaluation.
Overall residual risk never evaluated Residual risk judged hazard by hazard with no evaluation of the combined effect and the benefits.
Reliance on warnings instead of design controls Labeling and training used as the main control where a design or protective measure was available.
Post-market information not feeding the risk file Complaints and field data collected but never reviewed for new hazards or changes to estimated risk.

Best Practice Guidance

Define acceptability criteria before the first hazard is listed Agree severity and probability scales and the acceptance matrix in the plan so evaluation is not argued case by case.
Analyze use, not only the device Include use errors and reasonably foreseeable misuse by working through real user scenarios.
Show your control priority reasoning Record why design or protective options were not practicable before relying on information for safety.
Schedule a risk file review on a fixed trigger Review the file after each significant complaint trend, design change, or field safety action, not only at release.

Never do this

Start hazard analysis before the plan and acceptability criteria exist. Rely on a warning where the design could remove the hazard. Judge residual risk hazard by hazard and never as a whole. Treat the risk file as finished at release and ignore post-market information.

Control priority

Reduce risk in this order: inherently safe design and manufacture, protective measures, then information for safety and training. A warning label is the last line of defence, not the first.

ISO 14971 and the QMSR

FDA has stated it does not incorporate ISO 14971 into the regulation. The QMSR requires documented risk management through ISO 13485 clause 7.1, and ISO 14971 is the widely used method for meeting it. Check the requirements of other markets separately, for example EU MDR Annex I.

Key standards

ISO 14971:2019 - application of risk management to medical devices. ISO 13485:2016 Clause 7.1 - planning of product realization and risk management. 21 CFR 820.10(a) - quality management system requirement under the QMSR.
