Data Integrity Investigation - Verifying Trust in GMP Records

A data integrity investigation is opened when an audit trail anomaly, an employee report, an inspection finding, or a routine periodic review raises a concern that GMP data may not be attributable, original, contemporaneous, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, or available, the ALCOA+ principles that underpin every quality record. Unlike a standard deviation investigation, a data integrity investigation must determine not only root cause but the full extent of impact across time, systems, and product, since the question at stake is whether the data can be trusted at all. This map details the pathway from detection through investigation, root cause analysis, and remediation.

ALCOA+ Breach Categories (Overview)

Attributable / Original The record cannot be tied to the person who generated it, or is not the first capture of the data.
Contemporaneous / Accurate The record was not made at the time of the activity, or does not accurately reflect what occurred.
Complete / Consistent The record is missing required elements, or is inconsistent with other records of the same activity.
Enduring / Available The record cannot be retrieved, reconstructed, or made available for review when required.

Key Investigation Outputs (Overview)

Investigation report Formal record documenting root cause, full scope, and impact assessment for the data integrity concern.
Batch impact assessment Disposition decision for every batch or record identified within the investigation's determined scope.
CAPA plan Corrective and preventive actions addressing the systemic process, technology, or governance gap.
Regulatory notification Field Alert Report or equivalent notification filed where the impact assessment warrants disclosure.

Data Integrity Investigation Quality Elements

Immediate Data Preservation Affected records, systems, and audit trails secured against further alteration the moment a concern is identified.
Audit Trail Review Depth Review extends across the full relevant time period and system, not only the specific record initially flagged.
Investigation Objectivity Investigation team maintains independence from the area under review throughout the process.
Root Cause vs Individual Blame Analysis distinguishes a systemic process or technology gap from an isolated individual action.
Full Scope Determination Scope extended to every system, time period, and product potentially affected by the same gap.
Batch Impact Assessment Every batch or record within scope receives a documented disposition decision, including released product.
Regulatory Notification Timeliness Reporting obligations assessed and, where warranted, filed within the required regulatory timeframe.
CAPA Systemic Effectiveness Corrective actions verified to close the systemic gap, not only to address the specific instance found.

Regulatory Text - Source Provisions

21 CFR 211.68(b) (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

Input to and output from computer or related systems must be checked for accuracy, and only authorized personnel may make changes to established records, with all changes documented and reviewable.

FDA Data Integrity Guidance, Q&A Format (paraphrased - refer to the official guidance for exact wording)

When investigating a data integrity issue, a firm should determine the scope of the problem, including which other data, systems, or products may be affected by the same root cause.

21 CFR 211.192 (paraphrased - refer to the official regulation for exact wording)

Any unexplained discrepancy must be investigated, including an extension of the investigation to other batches that may be associated with the specific failure or discrepancy.

Common Inspection Findings - Data Integrity Management

Illustrative of recurring themes seen across published inspection outcomes. Not tied to a single specific citation.
Audit trails not reviewed as part of routine data review Analysts and reviewers sign off on results without reviewing the underlying audit trail for edits, deletions, or reprocessing.
Investigation scope limited to the single flagged record Root cause analysis stops at the specific instance without checking whether the same system or user pattern affected other records.
Shared login credentials undermine attributability Multiple users operating under one shared system account, making it impossible to attribute an action to a specific individual.
Batch disposition decided before full scope is known Release or rejection decisions made before the investigation has determined the complete time period and system impact.

Best Practice Guidance

Build audit trail review into routine data review Require reviewers to check the audit trail alongside the result itself, not as a separate optional step.
Preserve before you investigate Lock down system access and export the original audit trail before any interviews or further review begins.
Ask how far back, not just how deep Treat every data integrity investigation as a scope question first, checking the full time period the gap could have existed.
Separate the conduct question from the systemic question Run the HR and legal conduct review in parallel with, not instead of, the systemic root cause and CAPA analysis.

Never do this

Allow continued access to a system under investigation before evidence is preserved. Treat a data integrity concern as a routine deviation with a narrower scope. Assume a finding is an isolated incident without checking the full time period and system. Close the investigation without a documented batch impact assessment.

Data integrity vs standard deviation

A standard deviation investigation asks what happened to a specific batch or result. A data integrity investigation must additionally ask whether the underlying data can be trusted at all, requiring evidence preservation and a full-scope impact assessment that a routine deviation does not.

Falsification vs system error

Deliberate falsification is a conduct issue requiring legal and HR escalation alongside the quality investigation. A system or procedural gap that unintentionally compromised ALCOA+ principles is addressed through root cause analysis and systemic CAPA without individual disciplinary escalation.
