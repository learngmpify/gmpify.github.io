EU Inspection - Verifying Compliance Against EU GMP

An EU GMP inspection is conducted by a national competent authority, such as an EU member state inspectorate, to verify compliance with EU GMP as set out in Directive 2001/83/EC and the detailed guidance in EudraLex Volume 4. Inspection outcomes are recorded as deficiencies classified by severity, a system distinct from the FDA's 483 observation model, and drive the decision on whether a GMP certificate is issued, withheld, or a non-compliance statement results. This map details the pathway from preparation through conduct, deficiency classification, and certificate outcome.

EU GMP Chapter 1

Deficiency Classification (Overview)

Critical deficiency A practice or process that has produced, or leads to a significant risk of producing, a product harmful to the patient.
Major deficiency A significant departure from EU GMP that has produced, or may produce, a product that does not comply with its marketing authorization.
Other deficiency A departure from EU GMP that does not meet the criteria for a major or critical deficiency, but still requires correction.
Classification drives outcome The number and severity of deficiencies determines whether a certificate is issued, delayed pending CAPA, or replaced by a non-compliance statement.

Key Inspection Outputs (Overview)

Inspection report Formal document listing every deficiency identified, its classification, and the required response timeline.
GMP certificate Issued once the authority is satisfied that deficiencies have been adequately addressed, valid for a defined period.
Statement of non-compliance Issued when deficiencies are not adequately resolved, with implications for the site's ability to supply the EU market.
EudraGMDP database entry Certificates and non-compliance statements published to the EudraGMDP database, visible to other authorities and the public.

EU Inspection Readiness Quality Elements

Certificate Status Tracking GMP certificate validity and renewal timeline tracked proactively across every site supplying the EU market.

Regulatory Text - Source Provisions

Directive 2001/83/EC, Article 111 (paraphrased - refer to the official directive for exact wording)

The competent authority of the member state concerned shall ensure, by means of repeated inspections, that the legal requirements governing medicinal products are complied with, including by carrying out inspections of manufacturing sites.

EU GMP Chapter 1, Principle

The holder of a manufacturing authorisation must manufacture medicinal products so as to ensure that they are fit for their intended use, comply with the requirements of the marketing authorisation, and do not place patients at risk due to inadequate safety, quality, or efficacy.

Compilation of Union Procedures (paraphrased - refer to the official compilation for exact wording)

Deficiencies identified during an inspection are classified as critical, major, or other according to their actual or potential impact on product quality and patient safety, and this classification determines the required regulatory response.

Common Inspection Findings - EU Inspection Management

Illustrative of recurring themes seen across published inspection outcomes. Not tied to a single specific citation.
Deficiency classification disputed without basis Site challenges a major or critical classification in the written response without referencing the Compilation of Union Procedures definitions.
Corrective action addresses symptom, not cause Response fixes the specific instance the inspector observed without addressing the systemic process failure behind it.
Certificate renewal not tracked proactively GMP certificate approaching expiry with no renewal inspection scheduled, creating a supply risk for products relying on that site.
Prior deficiencies recur across inspection cycles The same or a closely related deficiency appearing in consecutive inspection reports, indicating the earlier CAPA was not effective.

Best Practice Guidance

Know the deficiency definitions before the inspector arrives Train key personnel on the critical, major, and other deficiency criteria so classification discussions during close-out are informed rather than reactive.
Draft the CAPA response against the actual definitions Structure the written response to explicitly demonstrate root cause and systemic corrective action, matching the rigor the classification implies.
Track certificate expiry on a rolling calendar Maintain a forward-looking view of every site's GMP certificate status so renewal inspections are scheduled well ahead of expiry.
Review deficiency history before every inspection Check prior inspection reports for the site as part of preparation, confirming previously committed CAPAs remain effective.

Never do this

Treat a major deficiency response with the same urgency as a routine observation. Submit a corrective action response without addressing root cause. Allow a GMP certificate to lapse without a renewal inspection scheduled. Assume a deficiency classification without checking the Compilation of Union Procedures definitions.

EU deficiency vs FDA 483 observation

An FDA 483 lists observations without a formal severity classification at the point of issuance. An EU inspection report classifies every deficiency as critical, major, or other at the time it is identified, directly linking severity to the certificate decision.

Routine vs triggered inspection

A routine inspection follows the authority's standard risk-based cycle and covers the site broadly. A triggered inspection responds to a specific concern and is typically narrower but more intensive around that issue.

Key regulations
