FDA Inspection - Managing the On-Site Regulatory Review

An FDA inspection is the agency's direct, on-site verification that a facility operates in compliance with GMP and applicable regulation. Authority for inspection comes from Section 704 of the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, and FDA investigators conduct inspections per the methodology in the Investigations Operations Manual. How a site prepares for, manages, and responds during an inspection shapes both the outcome and any post-inspection regulatory action. This map details the pathway from continuous inspection readiness through opening, daily conduct, close-out, and follow-up.

Key FDA Forms (Overview)

Form FDA 482 Notice of Inspection, presented at the start of the inspection to establish the investigator's authority to inspect.
Form FDA 483 Inspectional Observations, issued at close-out listing conditions the investigator considers objectionable.
Form FDA 484 Receipt for Samples, issued when the investigator collects physical samples during the inspection.
Establishment Inspection Report (EIR) The investigator's complete internal report, not typically shared with the firm, that documents the full inspection and its classification.

Front Room / Back Room Roles (Overview)

Front room lead Primary point of contact with the investigator, coordinating requests and managing the flow of the inspection.
Scribe / logger Records every document requested, every document provided, and every question asked throughout the inspection.
Back room team Retrieves and reviews documents before they reach the front room, and prepares subject matter experts for interviews.
Subject matter experts Answer investigator questions within their area of expertise, briefed in advance on communication expectations.

Inspection Readiness Quality Elements

Continuous Inspection Readiness Facility and documentation maintained in an audit-ready state at all times, not assembled reactively upon notice of an inspection.
Document Request Log Complete, real-time record of every document requested and provided, supporting an accurate internal account of the inspection.
Subject Matter Expert Availability Qualified personnel available and briefed to respond to investigator questions within their area without unnecessary delay.
Daily Wrap-Up Discipline End-of-day discussions held consistently to surface and clarify investigator observations before they harden into formal findings.
Real-Time Response Drafting Preliminary response strategy developed while the inspection is still underway, rather than starting from a blank page after close-out.
483 Response Timeliness Written response submitted within 15 business days, addressing each observation with root cause and a committed timeline.
CAPA Tracking Corrective actions committed in the 483 response tracked to documented, verified completion.
Post-Inspection Trend Review Observations reviewed against prior inspections to identify recurring themes requiring broader systemic correction.

Never do this

Provide a document to the investigator without first logging it and having it reviewed by the back room. Allow an untrained employee to answer investigator questions outside their expertise. Argue with or contradict the investigator's stated observations during the inspection itself. Miss the 15 business day window for the written 483 response.

482 vs 483 vs 484 vs EIR

Form 482 opens the inspection and establishes authority. Form 483 lists observations at close-out. Form 484 receipts any samples collected. The Establishment Inspection Report is the investigator's full internal record and is not issued to the firm at close-out.

Surveillance vs for-cause

A surveillance inspection is routine and risk-based, covering the facility broadly. A for-cause inspection is triggered by a specific concern and is typically narrower in scope but more intensive around the triggering issue.

Key regulations

FD&C Act Section 704 - FDA's statutory authority to inspect. 21 CFR Part 211 - current good manufacturing practice requirements being verified. FDA Investigations Operations Manual - the methodology FDA investigators follow during an inspection.
